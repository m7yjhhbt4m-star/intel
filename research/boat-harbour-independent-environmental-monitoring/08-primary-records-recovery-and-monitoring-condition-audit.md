# Primary Records Recovery and Monitoring-Condition Audit

**Version:** 1.0 — 22 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Record the primary datasets and project records recovered during the third evidence pass, distinguish what is now available from what remains missing, and audit the binding 2025 Decision Statement against the earlier draft conditions and monitoring design.

## 1. Executive result

This pass materially closes several earlier evidence gaps. The most important recoveries are:

1. The 2018 Supplemental Phase II ESA is publicly readable, including sample IDs, UTM coordinates, depth intervals, analyte matrix, exceedance tables and laboratory-report identifiers.
2. The November 2020 Environmental Impact Statement (EIS), Volume IV, provides a substantial synthesis of groundwater, surface-water and marine baseline data. It confirms 43 groundwater wells sampled in 2018 (13 existing and 30 newly installed), followed by seven additional wells in the Supplemental Phase II ESA.
3. The full 2020 Northumberland Strait baseline paper is accessible in readable form. Its sample design and methylmercury exceedance results can now be described much more precisely.
4. The BGC 2021 independent technical review quotes numerical results from the otherwise difficult-to-obtain 580-page GHD pilot construction report and identifies important limits in scaling those results to full remediation.
5. The final 2025 Decision Statement can be compared directly with the draft conditions. This reveals a material distinction: the draft contained a separate groundwater follow-up condition (5.7), but the final Decision Statement does not retain that separate condition. The final binding conditions include drinking-water monitoring, but do not expressly require a stand-alone groundwater monitoring programme in the same terms as draft 5.7.
6. The final Decision Statement makes public reporting, follow-up programme development, annual reporting, record retention and Indigenous participation explicit. The independent environmental monitor condition runs from site preparation through closure; other follow-up programmes can continue through post-closure.

These recoveries improve the evidence base but do not amount to recovery of all original laboratory certificates, raw data files, full pilot logs, or current field monitoring. No new field samples have been collected.

## 2. Recovered record: 2018 Supplemental Phase II ESA

**Source:** GHD, *Supplemental Phase 2 Environmental Site Assessment*, 14 September 2018.  
[Readable PDF](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5fa9ac321d1bf94e1f13e118/t/5fb2fc6aa269ed475ca25ce7/1605565624084/Supplementary+Phase+2+Study.pdf)

### 2.1 Scope and sample design now confirmed

- The report collected 19 sediment samples, plus QA/QC samples, at 10 site locations and five Northumberland Strait background locations approximately 2.5 km east of the estuary.
- Wetland sediment was sampled, where possible, in separate organic-rich surface material (0.0–0.5 m below ground surface) and underlying clay/silt (0.5–1.0 m), with deeper step-outs where needed.
- Seven new groundwater monitoring wells were sampled near the Boat Harbour Stabilization Lagoon and estuary, using low-flow purging and sampling where possible.
- The seven-well programme reported exceedances for metals, VOCs and general chemistry. BHSL-MW-6 had a reported cadmium result of 0.49 µg/L against a provincial ecological marine criterion of 0.12 µg/L, and chloroform of 6.2 µg/L against the provincial human-health criterion of 3 µg/L. The report recommended confirmation sampling because these were isolated results not observed in other wells in the Phase II/Supplemental Phase II datasets.
- Marine-groundwater pH results were also outside the comparison ranges at several wells; the report's table reports ranges of 4.94–6.79 for selected marine-groundwater samples.
- The report identified a need for additional horizontal PHC delineation at Northumberland Strait sample locations NS-SED-1 to NS-SED-7.
- Deeper sediment delineation remained incomplete at FSP2-SED-8 below 0.9 m and FSP2-SED-9 below 0.85 m.

**Interpretation:** These are site-specific assessment results and exceedance flags, not proof of a plume reaching the PLFN production wellfield. They are also not a substitute for time-series monitoring after 2018.

## 3. Recovered groundwater baseline: EIS Volume IV

**Source:** GHD, *Environmental Impact Statement*, Volume IV of V, 17 November 2020.  
[Volume IV PDF](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80164/137222E.pdf)

### 3.1 Groundwater sampling inventory

The EIS reports that 43 groundwater monitoring wells around the Boat Harbour Effluent Treatment Facility were sampled between February and August 2018:
- 13 existing wells;
- 30 newly installed wells from the 2018 Phase II ESA.

The EIS reports exceedances of provincial and/or federal criteria for some metals and general-chemistry parameters. It says criteria exceedances were not reported for PAHs, PHCs, VOCs, PCBs, phenols, or dioxins/furans in that 43-well dataset.

Four groundwater samples were collected near the existing containment cell. Only one, SDC-EXISTING-MW-3, exceeded marine groundwater criteria for pH in the EIS summary.

Seven additional wells were installed in the Supplemental Phase II ESA. The report says additional cadmium and/or chloroform exceedances were identified at selected wells and were not identified in other samples from the Phase II/Supplemental Phase II campaigns.

### 3.2 What this does and does not establish

- It establishes that a substantial groundwater investigation was conducted and that the 2018 results include localized exceedances.
- It establishes that the report contains an interpretable historical baseline and well inventory.
- It does not establish current conditions in 2026.
- It does not establish contamination of the present PLFN production wellfield.
- It does not settle the hydrogeological connectivity question. The assessment's regulator comments describe fracture flow and a semi-confined/"leaky" aquifer interpretation; this uncertainty should be resolved with well logs, hydraulic testing, time-series heads and a defensible conceptual model.

### 3.3 Containment-cell groundwater monitoring

The EIS reports indicator-parameter data from the MW4A/B background monitoring well nest through 2019. Parameters include alkalinity, sulphate, chloride, sodium, potassium, ammonia, conductivity, TDS and TOC. The EIS reports no significant increasing or decreasing trends in the 1996–2019 indicator results at those background wells.

That trend statement is limited to the named background wells and indicator parameters. It is not a finding that every contaminant is absent or that every groundwater pathway is stable.

## 4. Recovered marine baseline: Chaudhary et al. (2020)

**Paper:** Chaudhary, M., Walker, T.R., Willis, R. & Oakes, K. (2020), *Baseline characterization of sediments and marine biota near industrial effluent discharge in Northumberland Strait, Nova Scotia, Canada*, Marine Pollution Bulletin 157, 111372. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.marpolbul.2020.111372.  
[Publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0025326X20304902) · [Readable full-text copy](https://www.academia.edu/109415308/Baseline_characterization_of_sediments_and_marine_biota_near_industrial_effluent_discharge_in_Northumberland_Strait_Nova_Scotia_Canada)

### 4.1 Sampling design

- Sixteen stations were sampled along two transects extending approximately 8 km northeast and 7 km north from the estuary mouth.
- Twelve sediment samples were used in the sediment-metal summary: six collected in July 2018 using a gravity corer and six collected in July 2019 using a Ponar grab in the near field.
- Sediment samples represented 0–15 cm. Stations were classified near-field, mid-field and far-field.
- Eight intertidal blue-mussel stations were sampled along approximately 8 km of the Pictou Harbour coastline.
- Thirteen adult lobster samples (including two field duplicates) and thirteen composite rock-crab samples (each composite comprising 6–8 individuals) were collected in July 2018.
- Sediment and tissue samples were analyzed by an accredited commercial laboratory. Sediment chemistry included metals, total Hg and dioxins/furans; tissue chemistry included metals and dioxins/furans. Methylmercury was measured in a subset of near-field and far-field tissue samples.

### 4.2 Main findings and important qualification

The paper reports sediment metal concentrations below CCME ISQG values in the samples analyzed, total mercury below detection in sediment, and dioxin/furan concentrations below the cited ISQGs. It reports that the sediment results did not show evidence of significant industrial-effluent impacts in the sampled Strait stations.

However:
- Methylmercury in lobster and rock-crab tissue exceeded the cited Canadian Tissue Residue Guideline of 33.3 ng/kg at multiple sample locations. The paper lists results of 37.3, 53.4, 41.8, 47.2, 57.5 and 39.5 ng/kg for samples identified in its text.
- These MeHg results were from selected tissue samples, not all sampled animals.
- The paper explicitly states that MeHg had not been tested in Boat Harbour biota, water or sediment in the earlier studies it reviewed, making the source of the marine-biota MeHg uncertain.
- The paper discusses natural geological sources and other industrial sources as possibilities; it does not attribute the measured MeHg to Boat Harbour.
- The study is a near-field baseline, not a full Northumberland Strait survey.

**Monitoring consequence:** Future marine work should repeat comparable species and tissue protocols where feasible, include MeHg and total Hg as separate analytes, and add regional reference sites beyond the near-field transects. The historical near-field dataset should be treated as a valuable comparator, not proof of Strait-wide conditions.

## 5. Recovered remediation-pilot evidence from BGC's independent review

**Source:** BGC Engineering, *External Technical Review – Final*, 29 January 2021.  
[Full review PDF](https://aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80164/141419E.pdf)

BGC reviewed the GHD *Pilot Scale Testing Construction Report* dated 23 December 2019 (580 pages), which was among the extra-scope documents BGC requested. The full 580-page report has not yet been independently recovered in a directly accessible form, but BGC quotes key results:

- Average dredged-slurry solids were 5.2% in Phase 1 and 2.4% in Phase 2, with values as low as 1.0–1.5%, compared with a 5% target.
- Low slurry solids could extend Geotube dewatering time and delay construction.
- Geotubes initially dewatered rapidly, but much of the volume reduction occurred over time. BGC cites a Phase 1 example in which the first Geotube layer changed from a bulking factor of 35% to an overall 28% volume reduction after four months (a net volume change of 63%, as described in the review).
- BGC considered the pilot sample count limited relative to the anticipated full-scale material volume approaching 1 million m³.
- BGC considered the treatment results promising but noted that full-scale performance could not be confidently extrapolated from the limited pilot dataset, particularly while final discharge criteria were still being developed.
- The water balance used HELP modelling, while site-specific leachate-quality modelling relied on a single underdrain liquid sample collected from MH-1 in the HHA study.

These are not findings that the pilot failed. They are evidence that full-scale solids handling, Geotube consolidation, treatment performance, final discharge criteria and leachate estimates required verification rather than simple extrapolation.

## 6. Critical audit: draft conditions versus final 2025 Decision Statement

**Final binding source:** [IAAC Decision Statement, 24 January 2025](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/evaluations/proj/80164/document/160592)

This is a material correction to the earlier records plan.

### 6.1 Groundwater condition changed between draft and final

The draft potential conditions included a separate condition 5.7 that explicitly required a groundwater quality-and-quantity follow-up programme, including specified contaminants, monitoring locations in the Site Study Area and PLFN wellfield, static water levels, and monitoring through post-closure.

The final Decision Statement does **not** retain a separate condition 5.7. Its final condition 5.5 covers sediment, soil, surface water, drinking water and ambient air. The final wording does not expressly name groundwater as a separate medium in that condition. Drinking-water monitoring is required during all project phases, but that is not necessarily equivalent to a comprehensive groundwater network with aquifer heads, screened intervals, hydraulic gradients, fracture-flow assessment and contaminant chemistry.

**Consequence:** The earlier gap plan must not describe the draft groundwater condition as a final binding requirement. The gap remains material. Obtain the final follow-up programme and any provincial approval/permit requirements to determine whether groundwater monitoring beyond drinking-water sampling is specified elsewhere. This is a priority question for the regulator and PLFN.

Sources:
- [Draft Potential Conditions](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/evaluations/proj/80164/document/159634)
- [Final Decision Statement](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/evaluations/proj/80164/document/160592)

### 6.2 Final binding monitoring and disclosure conditions

The final Decision Statement provides the following requirements:

- **Condition 2.6:** each follow-up programme must set out methods, locations, frequency, timing, duration, reporting, review/update frequency, thresholds for action/stop-work, mitigation responses and quantifiable endpoints.
- **Conditions 2.8–2.10:** follow-up programme details and updates must be provided before implementation; results and mitigation responses must be reported; the Mi'kmaq of Nova Scotia must have opportunities to participate in monitoring, analysis and reporting.
- **Conditions 2.11–2.14:** annual reports are required and must be publicly posted; the specified documents are to remain publicly available for 25 years after closure or until post-closure ends, whichever comes first.
- **Condition 3.4:** sediment transport/deposition modelling in the Northumberland Strait is required before dam removal, with multiple dam-removal scenarios and mitigation measures.
- **Condition 3.7:** follow-up monitoring of benthic habitat, surface-water quality (including TSS), and sediment-deposition extent/thickness in the estuary and Northumberland Strait embayment north of the estuary is required before dam removal and through post-closure.
- **Condition 3.6:** containment-cell leachate must be collected and treated; contact water in Boat Harbour must be treated as necessary before release to the estuary.
- **Condition 5.3:** the Human Health Risk Assessment must be re-evaluated before site preparation in consultation with Health Canada and other relevant authorities.
- **Condition 5.4:** country-food follow-up must identify and monitor vegetation, wildlife and fish, including shellfish, and contaminants of potential concern in relevant tissues.
- **Condition 5.5:** follow-up addresses sediment, soil, surface water, drinking water and ambient air. It does not explicitly list groundwater in the final wording.
- **Condition 8:** a third-party independent environmental monitor must be retained before site preparation and report on implementation from site preparation through closure. The condition does not itself extend the independent monitor's mandate through post-closure.
- **Condition 9:** an alternative-site advisory committee with PLFN participation is required; the containment cell must be designed so that it can be removed and decommissioned.

### 6.3 Independent-monitor scope versus environmental monitoring scope

Do not conflate the third-party independent environmental monitor under Condition 8 with the full scientific follow-up programme. The independent monitor observes and reports on implementation of the Decision Statement conditions. The separate follow-up programmes define environmental monitoring and can continue through post-closure.

For a genuinely independent environmental investigation, the monitoring design should not rely solely on the scope or term of the proponent-retained Condition 8 monitor. The project should seek access to the follow-up plans, raw results, QA/QC, annual reports and independent-monitor reports as they become available, while retaining a separate PLFN-directed research/governance process.

## 8. Immediate records-recovery targets

The next records request should focus on specific items, not broad requests for "all environmental data":

1. GHD's 580-page *Pilot Scale Testing Construction Report* (23 December 2019), all appendices, lab certificates, raw process logs and full data tables.
2. Full Phase II ESA (June 2018) and Supplemental Phase II ESA datasets in machine-readable format, with every sample's coordinates, depth, analyte, unit, qualifier, detection limit, method and QA/QC.
3. Groundwater well construction logs, surveyed elevations, screened intervals, hydraulic testing, water-level time series, raw laboratory data and all post-2018 monitoring for the site, containment cell and PLFN wellfield.
4. The final follow-up programmes developed under Decision Statement conditions 3.7, 5.4 and 5.5, plus the approved contaminant lists, station maps, action thresholds, endpoints and reporting protocols.
5. Clarification of the groundwater monitoring obligation: what final provincial permits/approvals or follow-up plans require groundwater sampling and groundwater-level measurement beyond drinking-water testing, given the deletion of draft condition 5.7 from the final Decision Statement.
6. The final revised HHERA under condition 5.3 and its supporting exposure assumptions, receptor pathways, country-food species and target levels.
7. Current containment-cell quarterly groundwater/surface-water monitoring, leachate volumes and chemistry, inspection/geotechnical reports, and historical annual records.
8. Complete 2014 pipeline spill investigation and all follow-up sampling and restoration reports.
9. NSCC AGRG 2016 lidar point clouds, bathymetry, seabed classification, ground-truth data, ADCP raw time series, hydrodynamic model files and permission/licence for reuse.
10. Current legal and project status records, including any post-March-2026 judicial review update, and any subsequent annual report or public follow-up programme publication.

## 10. Bottom line

The research is no longer at the stage of merely knowing that the missing records probably exist. Several underlying datasets and technical reports are now directly accessible, and the remaining unknowns have been narrowed to specific files, fields and decisions.

The most consequential remaining blindspot is the **final groundwater monitoring framework**: the draft decision explicitly proposed a groundwater programme, but the final binding Decision Statement does not contain that separate condition. The final follow-up programme, provincial approvals and actual groundwater monitoring records must be obtained before the adequacy of groundwater protection can be judged.
