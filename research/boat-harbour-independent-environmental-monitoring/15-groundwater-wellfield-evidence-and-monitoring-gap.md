## 1. Purpose

This document examines the disputed groundwater pathway between the Boat Harbour site, the existing containment cell, shallow groundwater/surface water, deeper bedrock and the Pictou Landing First Nation (PLFN) off-peninsula drinking-water wellfield.

It brings together:
- the EIS groundwater baseline and its cited consultant studies;
- the external reviewer concern recorded in IAAC Information Requirement IAAC-67;
- the proponent's response in the consolidated Round 1 IR record;
- PLFN's EXP technical review and Hive IR82 analysis;
- the January 2025 IAAC Environmental Assessment Report;
- the October 2024 draft potential conditions; and
- the January 2025 final Decision Statement.

The purpose is to identify what is established, what is asserted, what remains disputed, and what an independent monitoring programme must be capable of testing. It does not determine whether the wellfield is contaminated, whether a hydraulic connection exists, or whether the final conditions are legally sufficient.

## 3. What the EIS says about baseline groundwater

The EIS states that baseline groundwater conditions were assessed using:
- public data, well records, reports and groundwater quality/quantity data;
- site-specific reports and geologic cross-sections/maps from earlier drilling;
- a groundwater monitoring network at the Boat Harbour site and PLFN wellfield; and
- additional regional water-well records.

The EIS describes three main monitoring areas:
1. Boat Harbour Effluent Treatment Facility (BHETF), primarily overburden and shallow bedrock;
2. the new off-peninsula PLFN wellfield, with wells at different depths to investigate deep bedrock and interactions; and
3. the old PLFN wellfield.

The EIS says a 2018 hydrogeological investigation included 30 wells installed during the Phase II ESA and seven additional wells during the Supplemental Phase II ESA. It reports a total of 50 monitoring wells on the site. The exact number sampled, the number of wells relevant to each hydrostratigraphic unit and the locations/screen intervals must be checked against the original well inventory and raw records.

### 3.1 Reported groundwater-quality exceedances

IAAC's January 2025 report summarizes the EIS baseline as follows:
- Most wells within the Site Study Area sampled in 2017 and 2018 met Health Canada's drinking-water guidelines.
- Wells directly adjacent to the Aeration Stabilization Basin had exceedances of aluminum, arsenic, cobalt, lead and/or chloride.
- Manganese exceeded drinking-water guidelines in several Site Study Area monitoring wells.
- The proponent attributed elevated manganese to naturally elevated background in Nova Scotia.
- Manganese measured in peninsula and off-peninsula wells between 2007 and 2010 was reported as consistent with the concentrations measured in the Site Study Area.

These are historical measurements summarized by IAAC. They do not establish current PLFN drinking-water contamination, exposure, or a source attribution to Boat Harbour. They do establish that groundwater quality near the ASB was not uniformly below all drinking-water screening criteria in the 2017–2018 dataset.

### 3.2 The proponent's wellfield position

The proponent stated that PLFN obtains drinking water from an off-peninsula groundwater wellfield east of the Site Study Area and that it is not hydraulically connected to the Site Study Area groundwater. IAAC's report describes the wellfield as over 500 metres east of the Site Study Area. Hive's report describes the wellfield as approximately 2 kilometres from the active containment cell.

These distances use different reference points (Site Study Area versus active cell) and should not be called inconsistent without checking the maps and measurement points.

The proponent's position is that the deeper aquifer supplying PLFN's wellfield is distinct from the shallow groundwater system near Boat Harbour and that project dewatering is not expected to affect the wellfield.

## 4. The central technical dispute — IAAC-67

The 2022 consolidated Round 1 response identifies IAAC-67 as an external reviewer issue involving DFO and Nova Scotia Environment. The context/rationale records that the hydrostratigraphic conceptual model in the EIS was unclear and that two studies appeared to provide different views of groundwater source and potential project effects on the PLFN wellfield.

### 4.1 External reviewer concern

The IAAC-67 rationale states:

- AECOM's 2016 Boat Harbour hydrogeology assessment indicated that the PLFN off-peninsula wellfield source capture zone was hydraulically connected to precipitation recharge and that changes in wellfield groundwater levels could occur, although relatively small.
- The EIS described a downward vertical hydraulic gradient between overburden or shallow bedrock and deep bedrock at the PLFN wellfield.
- GHD's 2018 Well Field Evaluation Report stated that there was no direct hydraulic connection between overburden/shallow bedrock and the deeper PLFN wellfield and that remediation dewatering would not affect wellfield groundwater quality or quantity.
- The reviewer stated that the EIS did not provide evidence of an effective stratigraphic confining layer to support the conceptual model's assumed limitation on vertical hydraulic conductivity.
- The EIS said there was limited surface-water/groundwater interaction, but did not specify which watercourses or the locations of those interactions.

The reviewer requested a detailed conceptual hydrostratigraphic model using all available information to:
- evaluate the off-peninsula wellfield capture zone;
- describe model-layer infiltration and vertical/horizontal conductivity and flow;
- describe the confining layer, if present;
- evaluate the potential for the Project to lower groundwater levels; and
- identify surface-water/groundwater interaction locations and update effects assessments if required.

This is an official technical-review concern recorded in the proponent's consolidated response. It is not itself proof that a connection exists.

### 4.2 The proponent's response — important admissions and assumptions

The proponent's IAAC-67 response states:

- shallow groundwater (less than approximately 10 metres) interacts with surface water;
- deeper groundwater (25 metres or more) supplies the PLFN off-peninsula wellfield;
- the monitoring-well network was not extensive enough to accurately map the wellfield capture zone, and the limited number of measurements further limited its utility;
- pumping tests had been completed to determine aquifer characteristics and information on hydraulic connections, but they were not designed to determine the capture-zone extent;
- a one-dimensional equation was considered unreliable because its assumptions generally do not apply to fractured-bedrock aquifers;
- the best available tool to evaluate the capture zone was the numerical groundwater-flow model developed by AMEC/AECOM in 2016;
- the modelled capture zones for active wells PW9 and PW10, at a combined pumping rate reported as 168 m³/day, were up to 300 metres wide and extended eastward from the wellheads toward the watershed boundary;
- the modelled capture zones did not extend west of the wellfield and did not underlie Boat Harbour;
- GHD reviewed the numerical model results and considered the predictions compatible with known hydrogeological conditions and pumping-test results.

The response describes three principal hydrogeological units: overburden, shallow bedrock and deep bedrock. It states that horizontal groundwater flow in overburden and shallow bedrock is generally from the surrounding highlands toward Boat Harbour. All overburden monitoring-well elevations adjacent to Boat Harbour were reported higher than Boat Harbour's surface-water elevation, indicating shallow groundwater discharge into Boat Harbour. The response also says a component of that flow is directed downward into deeper bedrock.

The proponent identifies a finer-grained layer near the top of the bedrock sequence, described in logs as siltstone, mudstone or shale, above the sandstone containing PW9 and PW10. It states that **there are no direct measurements of hydraulic conductivity for this finer-grained bedrock layer**. Lower conductivity is inferred from lower primary porosity and the expectation that the finer-grained rock may be less susceptible to fracturing than deeper sandstone.

The response further says monitoring wells MW-4, MW-5 and MW-6, completed in the mudstone/shale layer, did not show distinct fluctuations in response to routine extraction or the May 2018 pumping test that were evident in deeper than 50-metre sandstone wells. GHD interpreted this as indicating a disconnect between the deep groundwater in the pumping-well sandstone and the shallower wells.

### 4.3 What this does and does not resolve

The proponent supplied a numerical model-based response and an interpretation of the stratigraphy. The response also expressly acknowledges:
- the available monitoring network was not sufficient to accurately map the capture zone;
- pumping tests were not designed to map that capture zone; and
- the hydraulic conductivity of the proposed confining layer had not been directly measured.

The model's predicted capture zone not extending under Boat Harbour is a model output under its stated assumptions and pumping conditions. It is not the same as a direct field demonstration that no possible pathway exists under all conditions, including changes in pumping, seasonal recharge, fractures, future site conditions or containment-cell failure.

The downward component of shallow groundwater flow into deeper bedrock, the semi-confined/leaky description in IAAC's final report, and the absence of direct hydraulic-conductivity measurements for the confining layer are all material to the conceptual model. They do not prove a contaminant pathway to the wellfield; they identify assumptions and uncertainties that must be tested.

## 5. PLFN, Health Canada and provincial regulator positions

IAAC's 2025 Environmental Assessment Report records that the proponent excluded potable groundwater as an exposure pathway because it was not currently consumed within the Site Study Area and because the PLFN wellfield was said not to be hydraulically connected to Site Study Area groundwater.

Health Canada, Nova Scotia Environment and Climate Change, and PLFN did not agree with excluding potable groundwater as an exposure pathway solely on the basis of lack of current use.

The report records that:
- the provincial approval process would require adherence to the intent of the Nova Scotia Contaminated Sites Regulations and that the Site Study Area would be considered potable because it is not municipally serviced;
- a potable-water exclusion zone could restrict future installation of potable wells near the ASB and containment cell;
- PLFN expressed concern that such a zone could restrict future community expansion;
- PLFN also expressed concern about the effect of the containment cell on the current groundwater supply;
- Nova Scotia Environment and Climate Change described the PLFN groundwater supply as not being in a fully confined aquifer zone because of fracture flow, and as semi-confined or “leaky”;
- the Province stated that this did not necessarily mean the wellfield was at increased risk from planned remediation, but that uncertainty could be addressed through robust long-term groundwater monitoring; and
- the proponent committed to monitoring groundwater quality and quantity during all project phases, including post-closure.

IAAC agreed with Health Canada's and Nova Scotia Environment and Climate Change's view that potable groundwater should not have been deemed an inoperable exposure pathway in the HHERA. IAAC stated that drinking-water monitoring during all phases, including post-closure, would help ensure the Project did not negatively affect the PLFN wellfield.

These are distinct positions:
- PLFN's concern about current and future use of groundwater;
- Health Canada's and the Province's concern about pathway screening;
- the proponent's model-based position that the wellfield is not hydraulically connected to the Site Study Area; and
- IAAC's assessment that potable groundwater should remain an operable exposure pathway and that monitoring is needed.

## 6. Draft versus final federal conditions — a material change

### 6.1 October 2024 draft condition 5.7

The draft potential conditions included a specific condition 5.7 requiring a follow-up program for drinking-water quality and quantity. It expressly required:
- identifying contaminants including fluoride, nitrogen, total suspended solids, phenols, dioxins and furans;
- identifying groundwater-quality and quantity monitoring locations within the Site Study Area, including the PLFN wellfield and at minimum specified wells identified in EIS figures 7.1-12 and 7.1-14;
- monitoring groundwater quality and static water levels before site preparation and through all project phases and post-closure; and
- implementing modified/additional measures if monitored contaminants exceeded Health Canada's drinking-water guideline thresholds.

The draft also included a groundwater monitoring requirement in draft condition 5.3, which contemplated post-closure monitoring of soil, sediment, country foods, air, groundwater and surface water for the Human Health Risk Assessment.

### 6.2 January 2025 final Decision Statement

The final Decision Statement reorganized and narrowed the relevant wording:

- Final condition 5.3 requires re-evaluation of the Human Health Risk Assessment before site preparation and consultation on contaminants, receptors and operable exposure pathways. It refers to monitoring conducted under final conditions 5.4.3 and 5.5.2.
- Final condition 5.4 requires country-food follow-up monitoring.
- Final condition 5.5 requires follow-up monitoring for sediment, soil, surface water, drinking water and ambient air.
- Final condition 5.5.1 identifies contaminants and locations for those listed media.
- Final condition 5.5.2 requires baseline monitoring of sediment, soil, drinking water and ambient air.
- Final condition 5.5.3 requires post-closure monitoring of sediment, soil and surface water.
- Final condition 5.5.4 requires ambient-air and drinking-water monitoring during all phases.

The final Decision Statement does not use the term “groundwater” in its conditions and does not reproduce the draft standalone condition 5.7 requiring groundwater quality, quantity and static water-level monitoring at the PLFN wellfield and specified monitoring wells.

### 6.3 Precise conclusion

**There is no express standalone groundwater monitoring condition in the January 2025 federal Decision Statement wording reviewed.** Final condition 5.5 does expressly require drinking-water monitoring, including during all project phases, but it does not expressly require groundwater sampling or static groundwater-level monitoring.

This is not proof that no groundwater monitoring is legally required anywhere. Requirements may arise from:
- the current provincial Industrial Approval;
- amended permits or approvals;
- approved Environmental Management Plan / Project Environmental Protection Plan;
- other provincial statutory requirements;
- a separate agreement or commitment; or
- subsequent regulatory directions.

Those instruments have not yet been obtained and checked against the final federal Decision Statement. The proponent's groundwater-monitoring commitment recorded in the IAAC report must not be assumed to be an enforceable condition unless the binding instrument or approval that gives it effect is identified.

### 6.4 Why this needs counsel and technical review

The draft-to-final change should be analyzed against:
- the final IAAC report's conclusion that groundwater should be treated as an operable exposure pathway;
- the Province's statement that uncertainty could be addressed through robust long-term groundwater monitoring;
- the proponent's recorded commitment to groundwater quality and quantity monitoring through post-closure;
- PLFN's November 2024 comments on draft conditions;
- Health Canada's and Nova Scotia Environment's technical comments;
- the final Decision Statement and any provincial approvals.

Do not conclude that removal of draft condition 5.7 was unlawful or that the final Decision Statement is legally inadequate. Those are legal conclusions for counsel. The factual difference in wording is established and should be preserved as an issue.

## 8. Independent monitoring design implications

The independent monitoring programme should be capable of testing the conceptual model rather than simply repeating the proponent's conclusion.

A qualified hydrogeologist should prepare the final design. The following are proposed requirements for that design, not a pre-approved sampling plan.

### 8.1 Conceptual site model

Map and distinguish:
- containment-cell base, liner, leachate collection and underdrain system;
- ASB and other historical source areas;
- shallow overburden groundwater;
- shallow fractured bedrock;
- finer-grained siltstone/mudstone/shale interval proposed as a confining layer;
- deeper sandstone aquifer supplying PW9/PW10 and the PLFN off-peninsula wellfield;
- groundwater discharge to Boat Harbour, wetlands, streams and other surface-water features;
- the wellfield capture zone under current and reasonable future pumping conditions; and
- alternative pathways associated with containment-cell failure, elevated groundwater, leakage, spills and changes to pumping.

Every boundary and pathway should be labelled as measured, modelled, inferred or unknown.

### 8.2 Baseline and sentinel monitoring

Subject to access, rights-holder direction and expert design, consider:
- nested or multi-level monitoring wells at source, intermediate and receptor locations;
- monitoring at/near the cell and ASB;
- sentinel wells between the containment cell/Boat Harbour and the PLFN wellfield;
- monitoring of both shallow and deep units where technically justified;
- background/reference wells outside the expected project influence;
- PLFN wellfield monitoring, including production-well water quality and pumping rates;
- continuous or high-frequency groundwater-level logging at selected locations, with manual verification;
- synchronized surface-water level, tidal-stage and groundwater-level measurements to evaluate hydraulic gradients; and
- monitoring before, during and after any major dewatering, cell modification, dredging or dam-removal phase.

Do not select exact locations or screen depths from a map alone. Use the original well logs, elevations, geology, access permissions and field reconnaissance.

### 8.3 Analytical programme

The analyte list must be derived from:
- site-specific source materials and contaminants of potential concern;
- the final Human Health Risk Assessment and its underlying data;
- the draft condition 5.7 list and reasons for its inclusion;
- current provincial/federal drinking-water and environmental criteria;
- historical results and data qualifiers; and
- plausible source–pathway–receptor mechanisms.

The independent programme should not simply adopt a short list from one party without a documented rationale. Total and dissolved fractions, field parameters, detection limits and analytical methods should be specified where relevant by the qualified hydrogeologist and laboratory.

### 8.4 Water quantity and hydraulic testing

The programme should distinguish:
- static water levels;
- pumping water levels;
- recovery levels;
- seasonal recharge;
- tidal influence;
- pumping rate and duration;
- vertical and horizontal hydraulic gradients;
- hydraulic conductivity and storage parameters; and
- modelled capture zones under actual and scenario pumping.

A drinking-water sample from a production well is not a substitute for monitoring groundwater levels and chemistry in appropriately screened monitoring wells. Conversely, monitoring wells do not replace sampling water delivered for human consumption.

### 8.5 QA/QC and independence

Apply Document 12:
- project-specific SAP/QAPP;
- documented sample IDs, GPS coordinates, depths, times and well purge/low-flow procedures;
- calibration records and field measurements;
- field, equipment and trip blanks as appropriate;
- field duplicates and split samples;
- accredited laboratories with method/matrix-specific scope;
- complete custody transfers and seals;
- raw laboratory outputs and qualifiers;
- parallel review by an independent hydrogeologist; and
- prompt simultaneous release of results to PLFN, regulators and the independent monitor, subject to agreed governance and lawful confidentiality.

### 8.6 Predefined triggers

Before sampling starts, define:
- baseline and reference ranges;
- drinking-water criteria and site-specific trigger levels;
- statistically and scientifically justified change thresholds;
- response to a single exceedance versus confirmed trend;
- resampling and split-sample procedures;
- immediate notification triggers;
- operational pause or mitigation triggers where appropriate;
- investigation of source and pathway;
- requirements for additional wells or analytes; and
- duration/termination criteria.

Do not define thresholds after seeing the results. Do not rely only on regulatory compliance thresholds if those thresholds are not sufficient to detect early migration from a source toward a receptor.

## 10. Findings limited to the reviewed record

1. Historical monitoring in 2017–2018 found several drinking-water guideline exceedances in wells directly adjacent to the ASB; manganese exceedances were also reported in several site wells. This does not establish current PLFN drinking-water contamination or a Boat Harbour source.
2. The proponent's position is that the PLFN off-peninsula wellfield is not hydraulically connected to the Site Study Area groundwater. Hive and PLFN raise concerns based on the 2010/2016 reports and the possible recharge-area relationship.
3. IAAC-67 records a formal external reviewer concern about the unclear conceptual model and apparently different conclusions between AECOM 2016 and GHD 2018.
4. The proponent's response says the available monitoring network was insufficient to accurately map the capture zone, pumping tests were not designed to map it, and no direct measurements of hydraulic conductivity were available for the proposed confining layer.
5. The proponent relies on the 2016 numerical model to conclude that the capture zone did not extend beneath Boat Harbour. The underlying model and source reports have not yet been independently reviewed.
6. Nova Scotia Environment described the wellfield as semi-confined or leaky because of fracture flow, while stating that this did not necessarily imply increased project risk and that robust long-term monitoring could address uncertainty.
7. IAAC's 2025 report records that the potable groundwater pathway should not have been excluded from the HHERA and records a proponent commitment to groundwater quality/quantity monitoring through post-closure.
8. The October 2024 draft condition 5.7 expressly required groundwater quality, quantity and static water-level monitoring at the PLFN wellfield and specified wells. That standalone condition is absent from the January 2025 final Decision Statement.
9. The final Decision Statement expressly requires drinking-water monitoring but does not expressly require groundwater monitoring in its conditions. Other provincial approvals or project plans may impose separate requirements; these have not yet been checked.
10. The current record does not prove a hydraulic connection, current contamination of PLFN's wellfield, or absence of a pathway. It establishes a material technical uncertainty and a need to retrieve the original hydrogeology reports and current monitoring obligations.

## 8. Provincial monitoring instrument recovered — Ministerial Order MO-60007

**Primary source:** Nova Scotia Environment and Climate Change, Ministerial Order **MO-60007**, issued 28 May 2021 to Nova Scotia Lands Inc.; Schedule A monitoring obligations commence 1 June 2021. Official PDF: https://novascotia.ca/nse/issues/docs/Nova-Scotia-Lands-Ministerial-Order-June-01-2021.pdf

This is a material addition to the regulatory record. It corrects any broad statement that no provincial groundwater-monitoring instrument has been located.

### 8.1 What the order requires on its face

The order is directed to Nova Scotia Lands Inc. in relation to the former Boat Harbour effluent treatment facility and associated works. Its Schedule A states:

- **Condition 29:** from 1 June 2021, sample and analyse the BHETF groundwater and surface-water monitoring network in Appendix B, Table 3.
- **Condition 30:** collect surface- and groundwater samples consistently in accordance with established industry standards and best practices.
- **Condition 31:** visually inspect the BHETF monitoring-well network during each field visit and maintain wells as required, with protective casing and security.
- **Condition 32:** the Department reserves the right to modify groundwater/surface-water locations, parameters and frequency, and require remedial measures.
- **Conditions 33–34:** NS Lands may not change monitoring locations, parameters or frequency without written Department authorization; a qualified professional must provide written justification and the request must be submitted at least 30 days before implementation.
- **Condition 35:** review groundwater/surface-water quality as data are collected and report any groundwater-quality change immediately to the Department.
- **Condition 36:** maintain records for all BHETF groundwater wells, including maintenance records, for at least 15 years.
- **Conditions 37–38:** submit an annual groundwater/surface-water monitoring report by 1 April of the following year, prepared by or under the direction of a qualified professional licensed in Nova Scotia.

The required annual report contents include field methods, current groundwater and surface-water networks, recommendations, well-inspection deficiencies and maintenance, current and historical static water levels, groundwater gradients and flow directions, current and historical groundwater and surface-water chemistry, laboratory certificates, spatial and temporal trend interpretation, and identification of adverse impacts/recommendations.

### 8.2 The named network and required parameters

Appendix B, Table 3 identifies the following BHETF groundwater wells:

- MW15-1S
- MW15-1D
- MW15-2S
- MW15-2D
- MW15-3S
- MW15-3D
- MW15-4S
- MW15-5S

The order specifies **quarterly** sampling. The listed groundwater parameters are general inorganic chemistry (including fluoride), dissolved metals, dissolved organic carbon (DOC), chemical oxygen demand (COD), total Kjeldahl nitrogen (TKN), total phosphorus, absorbable organic halogens (AOX), and water levels.

The same table specifies quarterly surface-water sampling at Point 1, Point 2 and Pond 2 for a broader suite including total metals (including mercury), chromium species, dissolved zinc, DOC, TSS, BOD, COD, TKN, total phosphorus, TPH/BTEX, PAHs, VOCs and AOX.

The order states that the monitoring locations are depicted in Figure 3-1 of Dillon Consulting Limited, File 18-7281-2000, March 2019, *Boat Harbour Effluent Treatment System – 2018 Monitoring Program*. That report is therefore a high-priority map and monitoring-design source.

### 8.3 What this order does not establish

- It is a BHETF site network order. The named MW15 wells are not the same identifiers as PLFN production wells PW9 and PW10.
- The order does not by itself demonstrate that the quarterly monitoring was completed, that results were compliant, or that the data were independently validated.

### 8.5 Analytical implications

The MO-60007 reporting requirements are particularly important for a time-based groundwater map because they call for historical/static water levels, gradients and flow direction, historical chemistry, lab certificates and spatial/temporal trend interpretation. If the required annual reports and raw records are recovered, they can anchor a dated monitoring layer.

Before mapping flow arrows or contours, verify that water-level measurements are from the same hydrostratigraphic unit and comparable survey windows, and that measuring-point elevations share a consistent vertical datum. Before mapping concentration surfaces, verify the station network, analyte, sample medium, units, detection limits, qualifiers and laboratory QA/QC. Do not infer a plume from the well points alone.
