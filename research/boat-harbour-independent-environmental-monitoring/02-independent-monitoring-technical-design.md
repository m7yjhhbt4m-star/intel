# Independent Environmental Monitoring — Full Technical Design

**Version:** 2.0 — proposed design framework, 21 November 2026  
**Status:** Technical proposal only. Not a field-ready sampling plan until rights-holder engagement, access, current project status, lab methods, station coordinates and statistical power are resolved.

## 1. Monitoring objectives

1. Establish an independent present-day baseline for water and sediment in Boat Harbour/A’se’k, associated wetlands, estuary and receiving marine environment.
2. Reconstruct historical contamination patterns and determine where data are inadequate by medium, analyte, depth, date and location.
3. Test for contaminant gradients and transport pathways into the Northumberland Strait without assuming migration has occurred.
4. Characterize sediment thickness, stratigraphy, depositional areas and potential resuspension pathways.
5. Establish repeatable seasonal and event-based monitoring that can detect changes through time.
6. Independently evaluate future remediation predictions and monitoring results, if project work proceeds.
7. Produce an auditable public evidence record with clear chain of custody, QA/QC and data provenance, subject to PLFN data governance.

## 2. Governance and decision gates come first

This work concerns Pictou Landing First Nation's territory, rights, knowledge, community and food systems. The programme cannot presume that an outside investigator can select culturally sensitive locations, sample country foods, publish Indigenous Knowledge or define acceptable endpoints.

Before station selection:
- Engage Pictou Landing First Nation (PLFN) and follow its direction on research purpose, governance, access, sensitive data, interpretation and release.
- Determine whether PLFN wants to co-design, lead, oversee or independently review the programme.
- Agree on data ownership, storage, access, retention, publication, review and dispute processes.
- Confirm access to reserve lands, private lands and provincial/federal lands.
- Confirm whether sampling could interfere with remediation work or existing monitoring.
- Obtain applicable drone, marine, fisheries, wildlife, wetland and sampling permissions.
- Establish conflict-of-interest disclosure and independent QA/QC review.

**Decision gate:** no field deployment until governance, access and permits are in place.

## 3. Spatial sampling framework

### 3.1 Use strata, not arbitrary circles

The project contains multiple hydrologically distinct systems. Use separate strata and retain their identities in the database.

| Stratum | Coverage | Main questions |
|---|---|---|
| A. Main lagoon | Boat Harbour stabilization basin; inlet, outlet, former discharge influence, depositional zones, western and eastern sectors | Current sediment inventory; depth profiles; water column; sediment thickness |
| B. Settling and treatment infrastructure | Twin settling basins, aeration stabilization basin, former settling ponds, ditches and channels | Source-area distribution; sludge and contaminant pathways |
| C. Historic wetland discharge route | Natural wetland/settling areas used 1967–1972; pipeline corridor and 2014 spill receiving area | Legacy pathway; 2014 spill footprint; wetland/soil/water impacts |
| D. Other adjacent wetlands | All identified wetland areas, prioritizing unsampled and hydrologically connected sites | Sediment and surface-water gradients; habitat and ecological condition |
| E. Estuary | Channel, marsh/salt marsh, tidal transition and dam/causeway influence | Tidal mixing; sediment erosion/deposition; water-quality transitions |
| F. Near-field Strait | Estuary mouth and shoreline, preserve comparable 2020 stations where possible | Repeat baseline; near-field gradient and deposition |
| G. Mid-/far-field Strait | Expand beyond the two 2020 transects; include alongshore and cross-shore positions based on currents, substrate and bathymetry | Test spatial extent and potential transport |
| H. Regional reference | Comparable sediment type, depth, exposure, habitat and hydrodynamic setting outside plausible site influence | Regional background and control comparisons |
| I. Containment cell and leachate pathways | Cell perimeter, groundwater monitoring network, drainage, leachate/contact-water systems | Potential containment-related pathway if/when expansion proceeds |

### 3.2 The Strait-wide question

“Entire Northumberland Strait” is a large regional ambition, not a practical instruction to sample every square kilometre. A scientifically defensible plan should define the receiving-water domain with PLFN and technical specialists, then use:
- hydrodynamic transport pathways and tidal circulation;
- bathymetry and sediment depositional/erosional zones;
- near-field, intermediate and far-field strata;
- habitat/sensitive-area overlays;
- historic sample station locations;
- reference stations matched by sediment and exposure.

The federal marine RSA is a broad assessment boundary, while the 2020 field baseline is much smaller. The survey must explicitly show the difference between assessment boundaries and actual sampled footprints.

### 3.3 Station selection

Do not set final station counts before:
1. digitizing all historic station coordinates and sample depths;
2. mapping substrate, bathymetry, flow and habitats;
3. identifying access restrictions and existing monitoring stations;
4. defining primary analytes and expected variance;
5. calculating sample numbers for the desired detection power and confidence.

Preserve repeat stations from 2020 where coordinate accuracy and comparability permit. Add stations to close documented gaps, not merely to create an even-looking grid. Use a spatially balanced random or stratified-random design within each stratum, with targeted stations at known source/pathway features. Keep targeted hotspot samples separate from unbiased regional samples in statistical analyses.

## 4. Water sampling

### 4.1 Field measurements

At each station and sampling event, record:
- date/time, GPS, coordinate reference system and accuracy;
- tidal stage, water level, current direction/speed where feasible;
- water depth and sample depth;
- temperature, pH, specific conductivity/salinity, dissolved oxygen, turbidity;
- oxidation-reduction potential where relevant;
- weather, wind, precipitation history, recent events;
- visual observations (surface sheen, colour, plume, vegetation condition) without treating appearance as chemical proof.

Use calibrated multiparameter sondes. Collect profiles where depth allows, including surface and near-bottom observations. In the estuary, account for tidal phase and salinity stratification.

### 4.2 Laboratory water analyses

Core water programme:
- dissolved and total-recoverable metals: As, Cd, Cr, Cu, Pb, Hg, Zn;
- total Hg and methylmercury as distinct analytes where justified;
- TSS, turbidity and particle-size distribution where applicable;
- pH, conductivity, salinity, alkalinity, hardness, chloride, sulfate, DOC/TOC and nutrients.

Targeted organic suite based on source history and previous detections:
- PCDD/PCDF;
- individual PAHs;
- petroleum hydrocarbon fractions;
- PCBs;
- VOCs (including chloroform where prior ESA findings support it);
- phenols;
- resin/fatty acids;
- cyanide, chlorate/chlorite and sulfide where justified.

Do not automatically test every water sample for every analyte. Use a tiered design: broad screening at selected sentinel stations, then targeted follow-up based on source/pathway evidence and screening results.

### 4.3 Collection and preservation

Before sampling, obtain written method instructions from the accredited laboratory for each analyte and fraction:
- container material and cleaning;
- filtration requirements for dissolved metals;
- preservation acid and field filtration protocol;
- holding times and temperature;
- sample volume and headspace requirements for VOCs;
- chain-of-custody and shipping conditions.

Do not decide dissolved/total fraction after collection. The distinction must be part of the field plan.

## 5. Sediment sampling

### 5.1 Surface grabs

Use a suitable grab sampler (Ponar or equivalent) where substrate and water depth permit. Record:
- sample recovery and penetration;
- sediment texture, colour, odour and visible inclusions;
- shell/organic content;
- water depth, tide and GPS;
- whether the grab was disturbed or incomplete.

Odour and colour are field descriptors only, not contaminant identification.

### 5.2 Cores and vertical profiles

The 2017 and 2019 historical reviews show a strong shallow-sample bias. New coring must deliberately resolve depth.

- Select gravity, percussion, vibracore or piston coring based on substrate, depth and required recovery.
- Pilot core recovery before setting final station counts.
- Photograph and log the full core before subsampling.
- Preserve discrete depth intervals; do not homogenize entire cores.
- Choose intervals after sediment stratigraphy is understood. A proposed pilot could use finer intervals near the surface and depositional interfaces, with coarser intervals at depth; final intervals require lab cost and statistical rationale.
- Record core shortening, deformation, smearing, recovery percentage and refusal.
- Analyze deeper horizons to determine whether contaminant peaks occur below the historical 0–15 cm bias.

### 5.3 Sediment chemistry and physical properties

At selected stations, analyze:
- As, Cd, Cr, Cu, Pb, Zn, total Hg and MeHg;
- PCDD/PCDF congeners and TEQ;
- individual PAHs;
- petroleum hydrocarbons, PCBs, VOCs and other site-supported organics;
- grain size, total organic carbon, moisture, bulk density and redox-related parameters.

Use dry-weight basis for sediment chemistry unless the method requires otherwise; report moisture and conversion basis. Include porewater or passive sampling where it answers a specific bioavailability question.

### 5.4 Sediment thickness and bathymetry

Use:
- single-beam or multibeam bathymetry for water depth and bottom morphology;
- side-scan sonar for bottom texture and potential targets;
- sub-bottom profiling where acoustic conditions and sediment type support interpretation;
- cores to validate sediment thickness and acoustic interpretation.

Sonar cannot determine chemical composition. It must not be represented as a contaminant detector.

## 6. Passive sampling and bioavailability

DGT passive samplers are a useful complementary method for selected metals and mercury species at the sediment/water interface. The 2020 Boat Harbour study provides a local precedent.

Use DGT when:
- the question concerns labile/bioavailable fractions;
- the target analytes are within the binding-gel/method capability;
- deployment time, salinity, temperature and detection limits are controlled;
- the results can be interpreted alongside bulk sediment and porewater data.

Do not use DGT as a replacement for total sediment chemistry, water grabs, tissue sampling or toxicity testing. Include method blanks, replicates, deployment logs and laboratory confirmation. Verify the anomalous MeHg summary value in the published 2020 table before using it as a numerical comparator.

## 7. Biota and ecological indicators

Potential biota:
- American lobster;
- rock crab;
- blue mussels;
- selected benthic invertebrates;
- wetland macroinvertebrates and dragonfly larvae;
- fish species selected with PLFN and regulators.

Design requirements:
- species, tissue type, age/size, season, location and sample number must be standardized;
- include reference locations and appropriate biological covariates;
- distinguish total Hg from MeHg;
- consider arsenic speciation if human-health interpretation is intended;
- use accredited tissue methods and reporting limits appropriate to guidelines;
- obtain permits and PLFN direction before collection, especially for culturally important species or country foods.

Do not infer food safety from a single total-arsenic result or a limited number of tissue samples.

## 8. Drone and remote-sensing integration

### 8.1 Start with the decision, not the sensor

Use RGB and multispectral imaging first for:
- shoreline and wetland mapping;
- exposed sediment and erosion;
- vegetation condition and change;
- surface plume/turbidity patterns;
- repeat orthomosaics and change detection.

Consider hyperspectral only after a pilot demonstrates a measurable benefit over multispectral imagery and concurrent field sampling.

### 8.2 What optical imagery can estimate

With local calibration and independent validation, optical data may support models of:
- TSS/suspended particulate concentration;
- turbidity;
- chlorophyll-a and algal indicators;
- coloured dissolved organic matter;
- vegetation stress and wetland condition;
- relative plume extent and change over time.

These are optical proxies, not direct chemical assays.

### 8.3 What it cannot do

- It cannot identify individual dissolved metals or mercury species in natural water at environmental concentrations.
- It cannot identify PCDD/F, PAH, PCB or PFAS concentrations from a drone image.
- It cannot measure the chemical composition of submerged sediment.
- It cannot reliably see through turbid water to characterize the bottom.
- It cannot attribute an observed plume to a source without paired samples and hydrodynamic evidence.

### 8.4 Calibration and validation

Each quantitative flight campaign must be paired with field samples across the observed range of conditions. Record reflectance calibration, irradiance, sun angle, flight altitude, overlap, sensor settings, tide, water level, wind, turbidity, georeferencing and processing version. Separate training and independent validation samples. Do not train and validate on the same small sample set and then claim general performance.

## 9. Sampling cadence

A proposed programme should combine:
- an initial baseline round across all approved strata;
- seasonal repeat rounds to measure seasonal variability;
- event-based rounds after significant rainfall/runoff or resuspension events;
- tidal-stage sampling in the estuary and near-field Strait;
- higher-frequency turbidity/TSS logging during active remediation or dam removal, if it proceeds;
- post-event sediment deposition and benthic habitat checks.

Exact frequency and station counts require a statistical design and review of existing data. A quarterly schedule is not automatically adequate; it may miss short-lived tidal or storm-driven events.

## 10. QA/QC and chain of custody

Minimum requirements:
- accredited laboratory with relevant method scope;
- reporting limits below the comparison criteria where feasible;
- unique immutable sample IDs;
- GPS coordinates and coordinate reference system;
- chain-of-custody records;
- field duplicates, equipment blanks, field blanks, trip blanks for VOCs, laboratory blanks, matrix spikes and certified reference materials at QA-plan frequencies;
- calibration records and pre-/post-deployment checks for sondes;
- core recovery and deformation records;
- detection limits, qualifiers, uncertainty and units retained in the database;
- raw files, instrument logs, lab certificates, chromatograms/spectra where available and processing scripts retained unchanged;
- corrected data and derived/modelled data stored separately from original data.

Do not convert non-detects to zero. Preserve censored values with their reporting limits and use appropriate statistical methods. Do not interpolate across distinct water bodies or substrate regimes without a defensible spatial model.

## 11. Data architecture and publication

Recommended open stack:
- QGIS for geospatial analysis;
- PostGIS for canonical station, sample and result records;
- GeoPackage for field GIS exchange;
- CSV/Parquet for analytical data exports;
- version-controlled analysis scripts and data dictionaries.

Each result record should include source ID, sample ID, coordinate, medium, depth, date/time, analyte, result, unit, fraction, detection/reporting limit, qualifier, method, lab, QA/QC status, guideline source/version and provenance.

Separate:
1. raw field/lab records;
2. QA-reviewed observations;
3. interpreted results;
4. model outputs and remote-sensing estimates;
5. public map products.

Publish the methods and metadata with the results, not just a colour-coded map.

## 12. Pilot and scale-up sequence

1. Obtain PLFN direction, permissions and current project constraints.
2. Recover historical station coordinates and lab data.
3. Build the GIS evidence inventory.
4. Select a small pilot spanning lagoon, wetland, estuary, near-field Strait and reference strata.
5. Collect matched water, sediment, field-sonde and drone observations.
6. Pilot cores and sonar, validating sediment thickness against core logs.
7. Validate lab reporting limits and analytical methods.
8. Calculate statistical power and revise station counts.
9. Finalize the full monitoring plan and budget.
10. Establish independent review and reporting before field deployment.

## 13. Decision gates

- **Gate 1 — Governance:** no fieldwork before PLFN engagement, access and data governance.
- **Gate 2 — Evidence inventory:** no final station plan before historical data and GIS gap analysis.
- **Gate 3 — Laboratory readiness:** no collection before containers, preservation, reporting limits and holding times are confirmed.
- **Gate 4 — Sensor validation:** no quantitative drone product without local calibration and independent validation.
- **Gate 5 — Statistical design:** no full deployment until sample numbers and cadence have a documented power rationale.
- **Gate 6 — Public reporting:** no interpretation without methods, QA/QC, detection limits and uncertainty disclosed.


## 14. Dewatering, Geotube filtrate and containment-cell monitoring

Do not treat dredging and dewatering as a black box. The project process moves sediment, lagoon water and porewater through slurry conditioning, Geotube filtration, water treatment and containment. Each transfer is a potential pathway for particulate or dissolved contaminants. A separate detailed analysis and proposed monitoring-point list is in [Remediation Process and Leachate Monitoring](06-remediation-process-and-leachate-monitoring.md).

At minimum, design the independent programme to measure both total and dissolved metals, TSS/particulate matter, pH, redox, conductivity and process conditions at source water, slurry, Geotube filtrate, treatment influent/effluent, contact water, leachate and relevant groundwater/surface-water points. The 2021 Boat Harbour sediment geotextile study supports monitoring Cu, Pb and Zn migration and filter-cake development, but it does not validate all analytes or full-scale operation.


## 15. Additional requirements from blindspot-closure research

The second-pass evidence review identified requirements that must be incorporated before this framework can become a field-ready plan. Full rationale and source detail are in [Blindspot Closure and New Evidence](07-blindspot-closure-and-new-evidence.md).

### 15.1 Groundwater and hydrogeology are a priority medium

Do not treat groundwater as only a containment-cell contingency. The 2025 federal assessment records 2017–2018 exceedances of aluminum, arsenic, cobalt, lead and/or chloride in wells adjacent to the aeration stabilization basin. Health Canada, Nova Scotia Environment and Climate Change, and PLFN challenged the exclusion of potable groundwater as an exposure pathway; IAAC agreed it should not have been screened out. The regulator described fracture flow in the PLFN wellfield aquifer and recommended a robust long-term groundwater programme. This does not establish that the PLFN wellfield is contaminated; it establishes a material uncertainty that requires independent evaluation.

Add a hydrogeological conceptual site model, full well inventory and raw historical database, seasonal groundwater elevations, fracture-flow assessment, groundwater/surface-water interaction monitoring, and PLFN-governed drinking-water baseline. Include analytes supported by the site record and compare results with appropriate drinking-water criteria without confusing natural background with industrial influence.

### 15.2 Country-food and food-web monitoring

The federal assessment records Health Canada concerns about screened-out country-food pathways, inadequate consideration of bioaccumulation/biomagnification, and the need to reconsider shellfish. It recommends extending relevant country-food monitoring beyond the estuary to address potential exposure from suspended sediment in the Strait. Work with PLFN to identify consumed/intended species, edible tissues, seasonal harvesting areas and monitoring priorities. Include species-appropriate total Hg/MeHg and total/inorganic As speciation where warranted. Do not assume any species is contaminated or safe based on a limited proxy species.

### 15.3 Marine methylmercury follow-up

The accessible abstract of the 2020 Northumberland Strait baseline paper reports methylmercury tissue guideline exceedances in lobster and rock crab, despite generally low sediment guideline results. Obtain the full paper and raw/sample-level dataset before using exact values. Make methylmercury a specific marine-biota follow-up question, not a general inference of regional contamination.

### 15.4 Reuse and update existing physical-oceanography data

Before commissioning duplicate surveys, obtain the 2016 NSCC AGRG topo-bathymetric lidar, seabed classification, 35-day ADCP record and outer Pictou Harbour model deliverables. The report documents limitations in current-model phase/amplitude, bed resistance, wind forcing and bottom classification. Treat it as a valuable historical baseline, not a current whole-Strait model. Compare with current hydrographic data and design additional seasonal ADCP deployments and model updates where needed.

### 15.5 Regional source attribution and reference stations

Map other historic and current industrial, municipal, watershed and marine inputs around Pictou Harbour and the Strait. The 2001 DFO regional habitat report identifies historic TBT in Pictou and other regional sources and habitats. Use this as a historical lead requiring current verification. Do not attribute regional contaminants to Boat Harbour without source-specific evidence. Reference sites must be matched for substrate, habitat, exposure and hydrodynamics.

### 15.6 Separate 2014 spill assessment

Treat the June 2014 pipeline rupture as a separate incident footprint with its own source, pathway and receiving-environment records. Recover spill investigation, pipeline failure, emergency response, sampling, cleanup, waste disposal and follow-up monitoring records. ECCC confirms the event and approximate release volume but its enforcement notice does not delineate current residual contamination.

### 15.7 Dam-removal model audit

Independently obtain and review the complete sediment transport model, model domain, calibration/validation, sediment properties, boundary conditions and scenarios. The federal EA records a proponent estimate of approximately 270,000 m³ of mobilized suspended solids and a predicted TSS exceedance period, while also recording DFO disagreement with the selected guideline basis and IAAC uncertainty about model predictions and geographic extent. Do not equate suspended-solids volume with hazardous sediment volume or contaminant mass. Model contaminant loads only when chemistry and particle partitioning data support it.


## 16. Design update — recovered baseline and final Decision Statement

The primary-record recovery pass adds several constraints to the proposed design:

- Preserve the 2018 Supplemental Phase II marine background stations BKGD-SED-11 to BKGD-SED-15 and the nearshore NS-SED locations as historical comparators where coordinate accuracy and permissions permit. The five background stations were sampled at 0.0–0.2 m in April 2018. They are not a sufficient Strait-wide reference network.
- Reconstruct the 43-well groundwater network sampled in February–August 2018 (13 existing and 30 new wells), then add the seven Supplemental Phase II wells. Obtain surveyed elevations, screened intervals and raw chemistry before designing replacement or repeat wells.
- Include MeHg and total Hg as separate analytes in the marine biota programme. The 2020 near-field study found selected lobster and rock-crab tissue MeHg results above its cited tissue residue guideline; source attribution remains unresolved.
- Treat the GHD pilot results as a warning against assuming stable solids percentages or rapid Geotube consolidation at scale. Record slurry solids, feed rate, polymer/coagulant dose, filtrate volume and chemistry, filter-cake age, and all solid/liquid mass-balance terms.
- Do not use the draft 2024 groundwater condition 5.7 as if it were final. The final 2025 Decision Statement omits that separate condition. Condition 5.5 names sediment, soil, surface water, drinking water and ambient air, but not groundwater as a separate medium. Before final station design, obtain the final follow-up plans and provincial approvals and resolve what aquifer monitoring is actually required.

### 16.1 Binding follow-up scope to incorporate

The final Decision Statement requires, among other things:
- sediment transport/deposition modelling in the Northumberland Strait before dam removal (condition 3.4);
- benthic habitat, TSS, surface-water quality and sediment deposition extent/thickness monitoring in the estuary and Northumberland Strait embayment through post-closure (condition 3.7);
- country-food monitoring that includes vegetation, wildlife and fish/shellfish (condition 5.4);
- follow-up for sediment, soil, surface water, drinking water and ambient air (condition 5.5);
- collection and treatment of containment-cell leachate and contact water as necessary before release (condition 3.6).

The third-party independent environmental monitor under condition 8 is a separate implementation-oversight role running from site preparation through closure; it is not a substitute for the full follow-up programme and does not itself extend through post-closure.

See [08 — Primary Records Recovery and Monitoring-Condition Audit](08-primary-records-recovery-and-monitoring-condition-audit.md) for the source-by-source comparison and data status.
