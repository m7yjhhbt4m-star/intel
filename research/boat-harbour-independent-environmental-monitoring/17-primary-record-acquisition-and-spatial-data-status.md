## 1. Acquisition status definitions

- **A0 — Located:** source identity or registry record located; binary not acquired.
- **A1 — Binary acquired:** original bytes downloaded; filename, byte size and SHA-256 recorded.
- **A2 — Preserved:** original binary stored read-only in controlled evidence storage with provenance and chain-of-custody record.
- **B — Text reviewed:** full or partial browser-rendered text examined; original binary not acquired/hashed.
- **C — Partial review:** excerpt, executive summary, index or secondary catalogue summary only.
- **D — Not obtained:** source identified but no original content acquired.
- **E — Unavailable/blocked:** access or retrieval attempt failed; reason and date recorded.

A source can have more than one status dimension, for example **B + E** (text reviewed, original binary download blocked). Do not call a source “acquired” when only a web-rendered text view or search snippet was read.

## 5. Formats for the documentary evidence database

Preserve and embed the following separately:

1. **Original source:** original PDF, TIFF, LAZ, LAS, GeoTIFF, CSV, XLSX, SHP/GDB or native model files, unchanged.
2. **Text extraction:** UTF-8 text or Markdown, page markers retained; OCR confidence and extraction tool/version recorded.
3. **Tables:** CSV for flat tables; XLSX only where multi-sheet structure is material. Retain original units, qualifiers, footnotes and page/table locators.
4. **Spatial vectors:** GeoPackage preferred for normalized working layers; retain original shapefiles/geodatabases unchanged.
5. **Rasters:** GeoTIFF with CRS, vertical datum, resolution, NoData and acquisition metadata; never silently resample the preserved original.
6. **Point clouds:** original LAZ/LAS with classification and CRS metadata.
7. **Time series:** CSV/Parquet with timezone, timestamp precision, station ID, units, sensor and QA flags.
8. **Database ingest:** PostGIS geometry plus JSONB provenance/metadata; store source-file object references and SHA-256 hashes.
9. **Map derivatives:** GeoPackage/GeoJSON for vectors and GeoTIFF/COG for raster derivatives; each derivative receives its own hash and processing record.

Never make a PDF-only copy and discard the native data. Never treat OCR output, extracted CSV or digitized map features as the original record.

Key results relevant to the Boat Harbour water/sediment evidence and the user's monitoring objective:
- The thesis reanalyzes eight historical studies from 1992–2015: 60 PCDD/F samples from 48 stations and 103 PAH samples from 85 stations. All 60 PCDD/F TEQs exceeded the CCME fish ISQG (0.85 pg/g); 40/60 (66.6%) exceeded the fish PEL (21.5 pg/g); 56/60 (93.3%) exceeded the 4 pg/g human-health soil guideline. 54% of PCDD/F samples and 69% of PAH samples were shallow 0–15 cm samples; deeper horizons were under-characterized. The down-gradient marine PCDD/F dataset consisted of one 1994 sample only.
- Five individual PAHs frequently exceeded effects thresholds. Total PAHs exceeded ER-L in 45% of samples but none exceeded ER-M. Diagnostic ratios suggested about 67% pyrogenic source, less than 20% petroleum combustion and 13.6% direct petroleum source. Possible pyrogenic sources included wood processes, coal combustion, local industrial emitters and long-range atmospheric transport; this is not definitive source attribution.
- PCDD/F sediment concentrations remained high despite the 1992 pulp-and-paper dioxin/furan regulations and no mill water releases reported to NPRI in 2011–2016. The thesis discusses possible legacy inputs from pulp bleaching before 1997 and/or chlor-alkali operations during 1971–1992; it does not prove the relative contribution of either source.
- Chapter 3's 151-core sediment thickness and physical-characterization study is also published as BH-205. It reports average black sediment thickness 26.6 cm (SD 12.2) and maximum about 45 cm, with the greatest thickness near the western inflow/deepest basin.
- Chapter 5's “field-scale” Geotube demonstration was actually a small 450 L test at one Cove A location: four 100 L batches plus one 50 L batch of 0.3% solids slurry, with 1.5% Solve 9244 polymer, through one 50×50 cm GT-500 bag. After seven days, average filter-cake solids were 14.0%; particle-count removal was about 99.7%. Particle counts were higher at the beginning of each fill, before the filter cake had stabilized.
- Chapter 6 used pressure filtration at 21, 35 and 49 kPa, bromide tracer transport and HYDRUS modelling. Higher pressure increased particle passage, especially before filter-cake formation. Both attachment and detachment were needed to fit the transport observations; exact coefficients could not be identified because hydraulic and geometric properties of the filter cake changed.
- **Critical analytical limitation:** the thesis did not directly measure PCDD/F concentrations in the filtrate. It estimated 2.69 pg/L from measured particle concentration and an assumed sediment TEQ (~400 pg/g), then compared that calculation to a 15 pg/L drinking-water limit. The author explicitly recommends direct PCDD/F analysis of laboratory and field-scale filtrate, long-term particle monitoring, and comparison with actual Boat Harbour field-dewatering monitoring data. Therefore the 99.7% result is particle-count removal, not a measured 99.7% reduction in dioxin/furan mass or proof that effluent meets marine discharge criteria.
- The thesis recommends establishing local organic and inorganic baseline concentrations in discussion with knowledge holders, including pre-1967 conditions.

**Relevance to inventory items 2 and 3:** this is independent research that helps interpret the sludge-thickness report and pilot testing, but it is not either original report. It must not upgrade those inventory items from C. The 151-core/26.6 cm research dataset, the 57-core/23 cm earlier paper, the 500-point SCG/WSP presentation and the >600-profile summaries remain separate surveys/scopes until raw station IDs and dates are reconciled.


**Independent-review gap confirmed:** BGC's January 2021 External Technical Review (BH-012) specifically lists the 23 December 2019, 580-page Pilot Scale Testing Construction Report as an extra-scope document it requested; it was not part of the received review set. Thus the BGC ETR did not independently validate the pilot report's volume-reduction, geotube-performance or construction findings. This strengthens—not closes—the need to obtain and review the original pilot report and raw data.
