# Boat Harbour / A’se’k — Initial Research Findings

**Research dossier status:** Version 1.0 — desk research compiled 21 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Preserve the source-backed findings already developed for an independent environmental monitoring investigation of Boat Harbour/A’se’k, adjacent wetlands and waterways, the estuary, the Northumberland Strait receiving environment, and appropriate regional reference areas.

> **Scope warning:** This is a desk-research synthesis, not a new field survey, regulatory audit, legal opinion, or proof that contaminants have migrated into any particular part of the Northumberland Strait. Each study has its own dates, sampling design, media, analytical limits and spatial footprint. Results must not be generalized beyond those boundaries.

## 1. Executive findings

1. **Boat Harbour is a documented legacy industrial contamination site.** The former tidal estuary was converted into an effluent stabilization facility beginning in 1967. The federal Impact Assessment Agency of Canada (IAAC) describes the facility's conversion and the remediation proposal in its January 2025 Environmental Assessment Report. The provincial project information identifies cadmium, dioxins, furans, mercury, PAHs, petroleum hydrocarbons and zinc among contaminants found in Boat Harbour sediment. [IAAC 2025 Environmental Assessment Report](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/evaluations/proj/80164/document/160559?culture=en-CA); [Build Nova Scotia — Boat Harbour](https://buildns.ca/projects/boat-harbour/).

2. **The historical sediment dataset is substantial but incomplete.** Hoffman et al. (2017) reviewed 103 Boat Harbour sediment samples from 81 stations and four reference locations, using secondary data collected from 1992–2015. Seven metal(loid)s (As, Cd, Cr, Cu, Pb, Hg and Zn) exceeded low-effect freshwater and marine sediment guideline levels in at least some samples. Six (As, Cd, Cr, Pb, Hg and Zn) exceeded severe-effect freshwater guideline levels; four (Cd, Cu, Hg and Zn) exceeded severe-effect marine guideline levels. Concentrations varied by period, with significantly higher Cd, Cu, Pb, Hg and Zn in 1998–2000 than in 1992–1996 and 2003–2015. **69% of samples were from the shallow 0–15 cm interval**, and the authors identified spatial, horizontal, vertical and temporal data gaps. [Hoffman et al. 2017, DOI: 10.1007/s10661-017-5952-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10661-017-5952-0); [ResearchGate publisher preview/abstract and figures](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/320894359_Spatiotemporal_assessment_quarter_century_of_pulp_mill_metal(loid)_contaminated_sediment_to_inform_remediation_decisions).

3. **Wetlands beyond the main lagoon are a distinct contamination question.** Quanz et al. (2021) sampled sediment, surface water and dragonfly larvae from wetlands surrounding the treatment facility. The study identified guideline exceedances for As, Cd, Cr, Cu, Zn and PCDD/PCDF in sediment in historically affected and/or currently effluent-exposed wetland areas. Surface-water exceedances included Cd, Cr, Cu, Pb, total Hg and Zn at particular wetlands. The authors found relatively low ecological risk to macroinvertebrates from current effluent inputs in the wetlands studied, while recommending natural attenuation with monitoring for some shallow areas and limited active remediation in deeper historically exposed areas. Those conclusions apply to the study's selected wetlands and sampling design, not every wetland or the full receiving environment. [Quanz et al. 2021, DOI: 10.1007/s11273-020-09779-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11273-020-09779-0); [full text hosted by Cape Breton University](https://cbu.scholaris.ca/bitstreams/ba722a33-5956-43af-926e-aa3be9791fb7/download).

4. **The Northumberland Strait has a baseline study, but it does not establish that the entire Strait is uncontaminated.** Chaudhary et al. (2020) sampled 16 sediment/lobster/rock-crab stations on two transects extending approximately 7 km north and 8 km northeast from the estuary mouth, plus eight blue-mussel stations along approximately 8 km of Pictou Harbour coastline. Sediment metals and dioxin/furan concentrations were below CCME Interim Sediment Quality Guidelines in the sampled sediments. However, methylmercury in six lobster/rock-crab tissue samples exceeded the cited Canadian tissue residue guideline (33.3 ng/kg). The authors described limited impacts/low risk overall but recommended further investigation of methylmercury and long-term monitoring. This is a geographically bounded baseline, not a full Northumberland Strait survey. [Chaudhary et al. 2020, DOI: 10.1016/j.marpolbul.2020.111372](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.marpolbul.2020.111372); [full article proof](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Tony-Walker-5/publication/342079949_Baseline_characterization_of_sediments_and_marine_biota_near_industrial_effluent_discharge_in_Northumberland_Strait_Nova_Scotia_Canada/links/5ee10b61299bf13a89101ad8/Baseline-characterization-of-sediments-and-marine-biota-near-industrial-effluent-discharge-in-Northumberland-Strait-Nova-Scotia-Canada.pdf).

5. **The 2025 federal assessment explicitly treats sediment resuspension and Strait monitoring as material issues.** The proponent's hydraulic modelling predicted approximately 270,000 m³ of suspended solids (mainly clay, silt and sand) could be mobilized by tidal action associated with dam removal. The assessment describes modelled TSS concentrations immediately after dam removal at about ten times the applicable marine guideline threshold, falling below it within 140 days in the model; armouring was modelled to reduce TSS entering the Strait by more than 50% compared with the initial model. These are **model predictions**, not measured future outcomes. IAAC required additional sediment transport/deposition modelling and follow-up monitoring in the estuary and Northumberland Strait embayment, including TSS, sediment deposition extent/thickness and benthic habitat. [IAAC 2025 Environmental Assessment Report](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/evaluations/proj/80164/document/160559?culture=en-CA).

6. **The project's legal and delivery status remains relevant to independent monitoring design.** Build Nova Scotia's project update page, in a March 2026 update, says the project team was working with Pictou Landing First Nation to assess an alternative to the existing approved containment cell, and that a judicial review of the federal environmental assessment approval, initiated by Pictou Landing First Nation in February 2025, remained ongoing. The same page states the federal approval was issued January 24, 2025, with conditions. Re-check status before relying on this as current. [Build Nova Scotia — project updates](https://boatharbourproject.buildns.ca/updates).

## 2. Site history and environmental setting

- Before industrial use, A’se’k/Boat Harbour was a tidal estuary connected to the Northumberland Strait and used by Pictou Landing First Nation. IAAC describes the 1967 conversion of the estuary into a closed effluent stabilization basin.
- Industrial effluent entered the system beginning in 1967. The wetland paper describes early raw-effluent routing through a natural wetland complex and Settling Pond 2; later upgrades routed effluent through two 50,000 m³ settling basins and an aeration/stabilization basin before discharge through Boat Harbour to the Strait.
- A chlor-alkali facility also discharged effluent into the treatment system from 1971 to 1992, as reported in the peer-reviewed wetland paper.
- Effluent discharge to Boat Harbour ceased on January 31, 2020 under the Boat Harbour Act. The facility was subsequently operated/maintained through project arrangements; the provincial project page should be consulted for the precise current operational and remediation status.
- The provincial project page says more than 200 studies have been conducted and lists cadmium, dioxins, furans, mercury, PAHs, petroleum hydrocarbons and zinc among sediment contaminants. This is a project proponent/government summary, not a substitute for examining the underlying study data.

## 3. Main sediment evidence — Hoffman et al. (2017)

**Study:** “Spatiotemporal assessment (quarter century) of pulp mill metal(loid) contaminated sediment to inform remediation decisions.” Environmental Monitoring and Assessment 189, 257 (2017).

- Dataset reviewed: 103 sediment samples from 81 Boat Harbour stations and four reference locations.
- Time span of underlying data: 1992–2015.
- Metals/metal(loid)s assessed: arsenic, cadmium, chromium, copper, lead, mercury and zinc.
- Guideline comparisons: Canadian freshwater and marine sediment quality guidelines.
- All seven metal(loid)s exceeded low-effect freshwater and marine guideline values in at least some samples.
- As, Cd, Cr, Pb, Hg and Zn exceeded severe-effect freshwater guideline values in some samples.
- Cd, Cu, Hg and Zn exceeded severe-effect marine guideline values in some samples.
- Cd, Cu, Pb, Hg and Zn were significantly higher in 1998–2000 than in the earlier 1992–1996 and later 2003–2015 periods.
- 69% of samples were shallow (0–15 cm). Deeper sediment horizons were therefore under-characterized.
- GIS review identified inadequate spatial coverage, making horizontal and vertical delineation difficult.
- Authors called for more detailed sampling and better background/reference concentrations.

**Interpretation:** This is strong evidence of historical metal contamination within the facility and of inadequate historical sampling density/depth for complete delineation. It does not, by itself, demonstrate present-day contaminant concentrations at every location, nor migration into the Strait.

**Important quantity caution:** The 2017 paper reports more than 170,000 m³ of unconsolidated sediment in its context. A later wetland paper cites estimates exceeding 570,000 m³. These figures come from different cited sources/contexts and should not be treated as interchangeable or as a verified current sediment inventory. The 2025 assessment's 270,000 m³ figure is a modelled volume of suspended solids associated with a dam-removal scenario, not the same metric.

## 4. Wetland evidence — Quanz et al. (2021)

**Study:** “Contaminant characterization in wetland media surrounding a pulp mill industrial effluent treatment facility.” Wetlands (published online January 2021), DOI 10.1007/s11273-020-09779-0.

- Twenty-five wetlands were identified around the facility; the study investigated five representative wetlands (WL1–WL5) and two reference wetlands (REF1 and REF2).
- Media: sediment, surface water and dragonfly larvae tissue (Libellulidae).
- Analytes included 25 metals, total mercury and PCDD/PCDF (dioxins/furans).
- Methods included ICP-MS for metals and high-resolution mass spectrometry (HRMS) for PCDD/PCDF.
- Sediment guideline exceedances included arsenic, cadmium, chromium, copper, zinc and PCDD/PCDF in selected wetlands.
- Reported sediment means included:
  - As: 7.0–10.0 mg/kg in WL3–WL5/WL1/WL2 compared with a cited ISQG of 5.9 mg/kg.
  - Cd: WL5 mean 6.3 mg/kg, above the cited 3.5 mg/kg PEL/Tier 1 value; several wetlands exceeded the 0.6 mg/kg ISQG.
  - Cr: WL1 and WL2 means 41 and 45 mg/kg, above the cited 37.3 mg/kg ISQG.
  - Cu: WL2 and WL5 means 40 and 69 mg/kg, above the cited 35.75 mg/kg ISQG.
  - Total Hg: WL1, WL2 and WL5 means 0.17, 0.31 and 0.40 mg/kg; the cited ISQG was 0.17 mg/kg.
  - Zn: WL1, WL2 and WL5 means 220, 250 and 705 mg/kg; the cited ISQG was 123 mg/kg and WL5 exceeded the cited 315 mg/kg PEL/Tier 1 value.
- Surface-water results included site-specific exceedances:
  - Cd: WL3 and WL5 exceeded the cited CCME long-term guideline.
  - Cr: WL3, WL5 and REF1 exceeded the cited 1 µg/L value.
  - Cu: WL3 and WL5 exceeded the cited conservative 2 µg/L value.
  - Pb: WL3, WL5 and REF1 exceeded the cited conservative 1 µg/L value.
  - Total Hg: WL5 was 0.064 µg/L versus the cited 0.026 µg/L long-term guideline.
  - Zn: WL3 and WL5 were 96 and 138 µg/L, above cited 30/37 µg/L short-/long-term comparison values.
- Tissue findings were mixed: elevated As, Cu and Pb relative to the study's reference comparison were found in some site wetlands, while reference tissue THg and PCDD/PCDF values were higher than near-facility wetland values. Composite samples and limited tissue amounts constrained statistical interpretation.
- The authors noted that total organic carbon, grain size and biological activity affect contaminant bioavailability; concentration-versus-guideline comparisons alone do not fully describe ecological risk.
- Authors' interpretation: relatively low ecological risk to macroinvertebrates from current effluent inputs in the studied wetlands; monitored natural recovery was suggested for shallow WL1/WL2 areas, with limited in-situ active remediation likely needed in deeper historically exposed wetland areas.

**Interpretation:** The study demonstrates why wetland sampling must be a separate workstream and why sediment depth, surface water, biological indicators and geochemistry matter. Do not extrapolate its seven wetland sites to all 25 identified wetlands or to all adjacent marshes.

## 5. Northumberland Strait baseline — Chaudhary et al. (2020)

**Study:** “Baseline characterization of sediments and marine biota near industrial effluent discharge in Northumberland Strait, Nova Scotia, Canada.” Marine Pollution Bulletin 157 (2020), article 111372. DOI 10.1016/j.marpolbul.2020.111372.

- The authors first noted that historical records were insufficient to establish a reliable marine-biota baseline because sampling locations, species and reporting were inconsistent and some key analytes had not been measured.
- Sixteen stations for sediment, lobster and rock crab were distributed along two transects from the estuary mouth: approximately 8 km northeast (NE) and 7 km north (N), divided into near-field (stations 1–3), mid-field (4–6) and far-field (7–8) areas.
- Eight blue-mussel stations covered approximately 8 km of Pictou Harbour coastline.
- Sediment sampling: six samples from mid/far-field stations in July 2018 using a gravity corer and six near-field surface samples in July 2019 using a petite Ponar grab.
- Biota: 13 adult lobsters and 13 composite rock-crab samples; composite blue mussels at eight intertidal stations.
- Sediment samples were analyzed for grain size, total organic carbon, As, Cd, Cr, Cu, total Hg, Pb, Zn and dioxins/furans. Metals were analyzed by ICP-MS; dioxins/furans by HRMS.
- Sediment metal and dioxin/furan concentrations were below CCME Interim Sediment Quality Guidelines in the sampled sediments.
- Methylmercury was measured in selected biota samples. Six lobster/rock-crab tissue samples exceeded the cited 33.3 ng/kg tissue residue guideline (reported exceedances: LN1 37.3, LN2 53.4, LNE1 41.8, LNE3 47.2, CNE1 57.5 and CN3 39.5 ng/kg).
- Total mercury and dioxin/furan concentrations in sampled marine biota were below detection limits in the reported analyses; the paper also reports arsenic tissue concentrations above its cited CFIA comparison in lobster and some rock crab samples. Total arsenic should not be interpreted as inorganic arsenic or as a direct food-safety conclusion without speciation and appropriate regulatory interpretation.
- Authors characterized the marine environment as having limited impacts/low risk overall, but recommended more detailed methylmercury investigation and long-term/periodic sediment and biota monitoring after remediation.

**Interpretation:** This study is an important pre-remediation baseline, but it is not an exhaustive Northumberland Strait survey. It samples defined transects and selected biota, with only 12 sediment samples and a limited set of biological samples. The independent programme should expand spatial coverage, preserve comparable stations where possible, and avoid declaring either widespread contamination or absence of contamination beyond the sampled footprint.

## 6. Supplementary Phase II Environmental Site Assessment

The supplementary Phase II ESA covered the treatment facility and associated areas including the Boat Harbour stabilization lagoon, estuary, Northumberland Strait, former settling ponds, mill air-emissions area, sludge disposal cell and twin settling basins.

Reported sampling included:
- Sediment: 19 samples at 10 site locations, plus five Northumberland Strait sediment samples.
- Soil: 20 surface soil samples and 14 subsurface samples from 13 drilled boreholes.
- Groundwater: seven samples from seven newly installed monitoring wells.
- Surface water: one stream sample and five background samples from Chance Harbour Lake.

The assessment considered metals, PAHs, petroleum hydrocarbons, VOCs, dioxins/furans, PCBs, resin/fatty acids and general chemistry parameters. Methods included gravity coring, percussion and Ponar grab sampling; hand auger/shovel and drilled boreholes; monitoring wells; low-flow groundwater sampling; direct-immersion surface-water sampling; and laboratory analysis.

The report describes exceedances of relevant provincial and/or federal criteria in sediment for metals, PAHs, petroleum hydrocarbons, VOCs and/or dioxins/furans; soil for selected metals and pH; groundwater for cadmium, chloroform/trichloromethane and pH; and background surface water at Chance Harbour Lake for aluminum and pH. The one stream sample downstream of the sludge disposal cell overflow pond was reported below the marine surface-water criteria used in that assessment.

**Critical sampling limitations documented by the assessment:**
- Seven planned boreholes/monitoring wells in Boat Harbour and the estuary were not advanced because access agreements could not be negotiated in time.
- Ten pipeline-corridor boreholes, one hand-augered borehole and eight proposed sediment samples were not collected because of the risk of damaging the pipeline while the facility was operating.
- GPS surveying of some wells was constrained by forest cover.
- A sediment sample at FSP3 could not be collected below 0.7 m because the underlying sediment was too dense.

**Interpretation:** The Phase II ESA is valuable because it spans several environmental media and areas, but it also documents material spatial and depth gaps. Its sample counts are not sufficient to treat the entire facility, adjacent wetlands and Strait as comprehensively characterized.

Source: [Supplementary Phase 2 Environmental Site Assessment PDF](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5fa9ac321d1bf94e1f13e118/t/5fb2fc6aa269ed475ca25ce7/1605565624084/Supplementary+Phase+2+Study.pdf).

## 7. Federal Environmental Assessment — January 2025

The IAAC Environmental Assessment Report is the principal current federal assessment source reviewed for this dossier.

Key facts:
- The assessment describes the remediation project as removal of contaminated sediment from Boat Harbour and nearby lands, with a proposed vertical expansion of the existing on-site hazardous-waste containment cell from 220,000 m³ to up to 1,074,000 m³.
- The proponent estimated that approximately 27 hectares of freshwater wetlands and 1.69 hectares of estuary would require remediation.
- The proponent's hydraulic modelling predicted approximately 270,000 m³ of suspended solids (mainly clay, silt and sand) could be mobilized by tidal action after dam removal.
- The proponent's model predicted average daily TSS immediately after dam removal at about ten times the applicable marine guideline threshold, declining below that threshold within 140 days. Armouring the estuary channel bed with gravel was modelled to reduce TSS entering the Strait by more than 50% relative to the initial model.
- These are modelled predictions and must be kept distinct from actual monitoring results.
- IAAC identified the need for erosion/sedimentation controls, sediment transport/deposition modelling for the Northumberland Strait, TSS monitoring, sediment deposition monitoring, benthic habitat monitoring and broader follow-up monitoring of sediment, soil, air, groundwater, surface water and country foods where relevant to potential health effects.
- The assessment describes the marine follow-up monitoring area as including the estuary and the Northumberland Strait embayment north of the estuary, plus sensitive benthic habitat locations.
- IAAC concluded the project was not likely to cause significant adverse environmental effects under CEAA 2012 after taking account of mitigation measures. This is a project impact-assessment conclusion; it is not a certification that the whole wider marine environment is free of legacy contamination.

Source: [IAAC 2025 Environmental Assessment Report, Project 80164](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/evaluations/proj/80164/document/160559?culture=en-CA).

## 9. Drone and remote-sensing conclusions

**What airborne optical sensing can usefully do**
- Map visible surface-water colour, turbidity plumes and suspended particulate patterns under suitable conditions.
- Estimate or map proxies for total suspended solids, turbidity, chlorophyll-a, coloured dissolved organic matter and related optical water-quality parameters when locally calibrated.
- Detect vegetation stress, changes in wetland vegetation, exposed sediment, surface staining and spatial changes in shoreline/wetland condition.
- Use repeated imagery to document change over time and direct field teams toward anomalies.
- Hyperspectral data provide many narrow spectral bands and can distinguish more subtle spectral differences than conventional multispectral sensors, but this does not mean that every chemical can be identified from its unique signature in real environmental water.

**What it cannot establish on its own**
- It cannot reliably identify or quantify dissolved mercury, methylmercury, arsenic, lead, cadmium, PFAS, dioxins/furans or most other individual contaminants at environmentally relevant concentrations in natural water.
- It cannot see through the water column to map submerged sediment chemistry. Water depth, turbidity, dissolved organic matter, bottom reflectance and surface conditions constrain optical penetration.
- It cannot prove a plume's chemical composition or source. A spectral anomaly is a screening observation requiring paired physical samples.
- It cannot replace sediment cores, water sampling, laboratory analysis or ecological assessment.

**Technical basis:** Jaywant and Arif (2024) review remote sensing methods for water-quality monitoring and explain that multispectral sensors have fewer, broader bands while hyperspectral sensors have many narrow bands. The review covers optical proxies and the importance of in-situ calibration/validation. Some studies estimate additional parameters using statistical or machine-learning models, but those are model estimates dependent on local training data, not direct chemical assays. [Jaywant & Arif 2024, Sensors 24(24), 8041, DOI 10.3390/s24248041](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11679694/).

**Implication for this project:** Treat the drone as a reconnaissance, mapping, anomaly-detection and repeat-monitoring platform. Pair every calibration/validation flight with georeferenced water samples and field measurements. Use a separate underwater/sonar programme for bathymetry, bottom morphology and sediment thickness; use cores/grabs and laboratory assays for sediment chemistry.

## 10. Contaminant programme — evidence-based target list

The following targets are justified by the studies reviewed; inclusion in a future analytical programme is a proposal, not a claim that every analyte is present at every location.

| Group | Targets to consider | Evidence / reason |
|---|---|---|
| Metals / metalloids | As, Cd, Cr, Cu, Pb, Hg, Zn | 2017 sediment dataset and 2021 wetland study |
| Mercury species | Total Hg and methylmercury (MeHg) | Legacy chlor-alkali history; 2020 Strait biota MeHg guideline exceedances; 2021 wetland results |
| Dioxins / furans | PCDD/PCDF congeners and TEQ | 2017 sediment study; 2020 marine baseline; 2021 wetland study |
| PAHs | Individual PAHs / summed PAHs | 2017 and Phase II ESA; provincial contaminant summary |
| Petroleum hydrocarbons | Appropriate fractions / TPH | Phase II ESA and provincial contaminant summary |
| VOCs | Site-specific VOC suite (including chloroform where supported) | Phase II ESA groundwater results |
| PCBs | Congener/total PCB suite where historical source review supports it | Phase II ESA analyte suite |
| Resin/fatty acids | Site-relevant resin and fatty acid suite | Phase II ESA analyte suite |
| General chemistry | pH, conductivity, chloride, sulfate, ammonia, sulfide, cyanide, chlorate/chlorite, nutrients as relevant | Phase II ESA and water-quality interpretation |

Do not automatically add PFAS as a Boat Harbour contaminant finding. It can be considered as a separate screening question only after a source/pathway rationale is documented. Do not add analytes merely because a drone manufacturer claims spectral detection.

## 11. Principal evidence gaps and unanswered questions

1. **Complete current sediment inventory:** What is the current volume, thickness, density, spatial distribution and chemistry of sediment in the lagoon, settling basins, former discharge channels and other site areas? Published volume estimates differ and are not directly comparable.
2. **Vertical delineation:** What contaminants occur below the shallow 0–15 cm horizon, especially in depositional zones and beneath/around historical discharge areas?
3. **Wetland coverage:** Which of the 25 identified wetlands have been sampled adequately, at what depths, and which have not?
4. **Northumberland Strait coverage:** What are the actual sediment and water conditions outside the 2020 study's two transects and eight mussel stations? What is the seasonal and storm-related variability?
5. **Mercury pathways:** What explains MeHg guideline exceedances in lobster and rock crab tissue? What are the sediment porewater, methylation, food-web and seasonal patterns?
6. **Dioxin/furan spatial coverage:** Are congeners and TEQs characterized sufficiently across all relevant sediments, wetlands and receiving environments?
7. **Groundwater and surface-water pathways:** What were the consequences of uncompleted Phase II boreholes and pipeline-corridor sampling? Are there additional seepage or runoff pathways?
8. **Baseline comparability:** Can historical station coordinates, raw laboratory data, detection limits, QA/QC records and sample metadata be obtained to enable valid time-series comparisons?
9. **Remediation monitoring:** What monitoring data have been collected since effluent cessation and what has been published under the federal follow-up requirements?
10. **Current project/legal status:** What is the outcome of the judicial review and the alternative containment-cell feasibility process? What construction or remediation schedule is now approved?
11. **Independent governance:** What monitoring design, data ownership, access, cultural protocols and public reporting arrangements does Pictou Landing First Nation want? This cannot be presumed by an external investigator.

## 12. Bottom line

The available literature supports a well-founded independent monitoring investigation. It documents legacy contamination in Boat Harbour sediments, contamination in selected surrounding wetlands, and a Northumberland Strait baseline that found mostly low sediment guideline exceedances in the sampled marine footprint but did identify methylmercury tissue guideline exceedances. The federal assessment itself identifies sediment mobilization, transport and follow-up monitoring in the estuary/Strait as important issues.

The evidence does **not** justify asserting that contaminants have spread throughout the Northumberland Strait. It also does **not** justify declaring the wider Strait fully characterized or free of contamination. The correct next step is to assemble original datasets and station coordinates, map the exact footprints and gaps, then design a stratified water/sediment/biota monitoring programme with independent QA/QC and laboratory confirmation.

## 13. 2025 Annual Report — actual implementation status

The 2025 IAAC annual report was subsequently retrieved and reviewed. This materially updates the status picture:

- The report states that the project remained in the **pre-construction phase** during 2025, pending the judicial review initiated by Pictou Landing First Nation.
- No project follow-up environmental monitoring was completed in 2025. No monitoring results, non-compliances or corrective actions were reported for the project conditions because construction and implementation had not begun.
- The report states that the fish and fish habitat, migratory bird, Indigenous health/socio-economic, cultural heritage, Indigenous monitor and independent environmental monitor conditions had no specific implementation action in 2025.
- Draft follow-up/monitoring plans had been submitted with the Environmental Assessment documentation, but revised plans incorporating the EA conditions were still to be prepared. Specified reports, plans and schedules under conditions 2.14 and 2.15 were not yet prepared/shared in 2025.
- The alternative-site process did advance: an Alternative Site Advisory Committee Terms of Reference was agreed and executed, and the committee met on November 20 and December 18, 2025. The report says a potential alternative location for long-term waste storage had been identified for feasibility analysis. It also records a January 22, 2026 submission indicating that a technically and economically feasible alternative had not yet been identified.
- The report says the project remained in pre-construction and that the judicial review outcome would determine next steps.

**Why this matters to independent monitoring:** The 2025 annual report is not a monitoring dataset. It establishes that no project follow-up monitoring results were generated in 2025 and that the independent environmental monitor condition had not yet been implemented at that time. Do not confuse “no non-compliances reported” in a pre-construction year with evidence that environmental conditions were measured and found compliant.
