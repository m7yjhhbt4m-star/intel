## 1. Scope and review method

This document records a source examination of four primary records and one additional Mi’kmaq community submission:

1. Pictou Landing First Nation (PLFN), *Final Position of Pictou Landing First Nation on Proposed Boat Harbour Remediation Project*, submitted 23 April 2024, IAAC registry comment 52, 54-page PDF.
2. PLFN, *Re IAAC Information Requirement Number 82 — PLFN Response to NS Technical and Economic Feasibility Assessment of Alternative Containment Cell Location Site*, Document Brief, 1 August 2023, submitted as the second attachment to comment 52, 48-page PDF.
3. EXP Services Inc., *Boat Harbour Remediation Project Environmental Impacts Statement Review*, 29 January 2021, 15-page PDF, submitted by PLFN on 8 May 2024 as IAAC registry comment 49.
4. PLFN legal counsel, letter to IAAC dated 14 November 2024, two-page redacted PDF, IAAC registry comment 62.
5. Sipekne’katik First Nation Consultation Department, letter dated 12 November 2024, submitted 14 November 2024 as IAAC registry comment 60, two-page PDF.

The full text of each listed PDF was accessible through the official IAAC registry and reviewed in the browser's PDF text representation. Page counts and document identities were checked against the registry attachment listings. This is a **full-text review**, not a binary forensic acquisition: the original PDF bytes were not successfully downloaded into the controlled evidence vault during this work session, no SHA-256 hashes have yet been calculated, and no independent second reviewer has signed off. These records therefore remain acquisition class **B**, not A0/A1, under Document 12.

This is a source examination, not a determination that the factual or legal assertions in the submissions are true. The submission is primary evidence of what its author submitted and the position it took. Underlying historical records, technical reports and legal authorities cited inside a submission must be separately obtained and assessed before their contents are treated as independently established.

## 2. Record A — PLFN Final Position, 23 April 2024

**Registry identity:** Comment 52. Submitted by an administrator on behalf of Pictou Landing First Nation. Two attachments: the 54-page Final Position and the 48-page Document Brief.

**Official registry record:**  
https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/evaluations/proj/80164/contributions/id/61897

**Direct PDF:**  
https://registrydocumentsprd.blob.core.windows.net/commentsblob/project-80164/comment-61897/Final%20Position%20of%20Pictou%20Landing%20First%20Nation%20on%20Proposed%20Boat%20Harbour%20Remediation%20Project.pdf

### 2.1 Authorship and nature of the document

The document is expressly titled PLFN's Final Position and is submitted on PLFN's behalf. It is a formal rights-holder submission containing legal argument, historical narrative, technical and alternative-site material, references to other reports, and community impact evidence. It is not a neutral expert report and should not be cited as if it were one.

The submission has 11 principal sections, including Aboriginal title, alleged infringement of section 35 rights, the absence of a remediation agreement, consultation, accommodation/compensation, alternative sites, hazardous-waste landfill siting criteria, and socio-economic and cultural effects.

### 2.2 PLFN's stated position

The document expressly states that PLFN does not consent to use of land it claims as Aboriginal title land for long-term storage of hazardous waste. PLFN argues that the existing and expanded containment cell interferes with the use and enjoyment of Aboriginal title lands, Indian Reserve 37 and Indian Reserve 24G, and that the proposed project should not be approved as designed.

The document advances several distinct legal contentions:

- PLFN asserts Aboriginal title to lands surrounding Boat Harbour, including the containment-cell location.
- PLFN argues that the existing landfill and its proposed expansion infringe section 35 Aboriginal and treaty rights.
- PLFN argues that the Province has not justified the asserted infringement.
- PLFN argues that a remediation agreement contemplated by earlier arrangements was not negotiated.
- PLFN argues that consultation before the Cabinet decision was inadequate.
- PLFN argues that proposed land transfers and compensation do not amount to adequate accommodation.
- PLFN argues that the Boat Harbour site is inferior to alternative locations under the Province's selected hazardous-waste landfill siting criteria.

**Legal characterization:** These are PLFN's arguments and asserted positions. The submission does not itself establish a court finding that title exists, that an infringement is unjustified, that a particular agreement is legally enforceable, or that the consultation duty was breached. Those questions require the underlying instruments, the complete litigation record and legal analysis by counsel.

### 2.3 Alternative-site argument — important but not yet independently validated

The executive summary and sections 8–9 state that PLFN commissioned Hive Engineering to identify potential Crown and private parcels and assess the former Northern Pulp landfill site at Abercrombie Point.

PLFN reports that Hive identified:

- 109 Crown-land parcels within 50 km meeting its desktop screening criteria, with 3 within 10 km;
- 97 private-land parcels within 50 km meeting the desktop screening criteria, with 12 within 10 km;
- the former Northern Pulp landfill as a possible alternative that Northern Pulp representatives had expressed openness to exploring; and
- nine siting-criteria exceedances for the existing Boat Harbour cell, while the Northern Pulp site was reported to meet the criteria applied.

The Final Position further says the site-screening criteria were selected by Nova Scotia Environment from criteria used in other jurisdictions and that Hive also considered some criteria not adopted in the provincial assessment.

**Critical limitations:**

1. These are findings reported by PLFN from the Hive report, not independently verified conclusions in this review.
2. The Hive report itself has not yet been retrieved and examined in full.
3. The underlying provincial alternative-site assessment, criteria, GIS layers, parcel screening rules, exclusions, groundwater data, surface-water setbacks, ownership/encumbrance checks and site-specific investigations must be reviewed.
4. A desktop-screened parcel is not a permitted, engineered, geotechnically suitable or environmentally approved hazardous-waste facility.
5. “Northern Pulp site meets all criteria” must remain attributed to the Hive assessment as summarized by PLFN until the full report and the underlying criteria/data have been checked.

**Next action:** obtain the original Hive Engineering report dated 4 August 2023, the complete provincial IR82 response and technical/economic feasibility assessment, and the source criteria used in the comparison. Conduct an independent, reproducible comparison using the same criteria and a transparent record of any additional criteria.

### 2.4 Nine criteria exceedances — issue list to verify

The Final Position reproduces a list attributed to Hive of nine siting-parameter exceedances for the Boat Harbour cell, including:

- separation between the bottom liner/leak-detection system and groundwater;
- distance to permanent surface water/wetland;
- distance to other properties;
- prevention of surface-water contamination;
- prevention of contamination in parks and wildlife/special-significance areas;
- groundwater isolation / accidental-release prevention;
- unsaturated-soil separation above seasonally high groundwater;
- separation from permanent watercourses/waterbodies; and
- distance to a potable water supply.

The exact wording and the source jurisdiction for each criterion must be checked against the Hive report and the underlying source standards. Do not assume that all nine are binding Nova Scotia statutory requirements. The submission itself says some criteria were borrowed from other jurisdictions and some additional criteria were not adopted by the Province.

### 2.5 Referenda

Paragraph 199 states that PLFN conducted separate referenda in 2021 and 2023 and that 98% of those voting in each referendum opposed long-term use of the Boat Harbour landfill for storage of contaminants from Boat Harbour.

**Established at this stage:** the 2024 PLFN Final Position contains this statement. IAAC's 2025 Environmental Assessment Report also records it as PLFN's statement.

**Not yet established from original referendum records:** the wording of each referendum question, eligibility rules, number of eligible voters, turnout, number of ballots cast, voting method, result certification, and any resolution or formal record. The document says a significant number voted but does not supply the full voting record in the passage reviewed. The 98% figure must be attributed to PLFN unless the referendum records are obtained and authenticated.

### 2.6 Historical and community-impact material

The Final Position includes historical narrative and quoted statements from community leadership, as well as assertions about health, mental health, trust, cultural continuity, economic and social effects. These are important records of PLFN's position and community testimony.

They must not be converted into epidemiological or medical causation findings. Any disease-prevalence, exposure or causation proposition requires separate, ethically governed health research, appropriate clinical/epidemiological expertise, and protection of personal information. Do not copy individual health details or quotations into the public repository beyond what is necessary to document the public submission's existence and subject matter.

## 4. Record C — EXP Services technical review submitted by PLFN, 8 May 2024

**Registry record:** IAAC public comments registry, reference number 49.  
**Direct PDF:**  
https://registrydocumentsprd.blob.core.windows.net/commentsblob/project-80164/comment-61832/Pictou%20Landing%20First%20Nations%20-%20Technical%20review%20of%20EIS.PDF

**Document identity:** EXP Services Inc., *Boat Harbour Remediation Project Environmental Impacts Statement Review*, dated 29 January 2021, 15 pages. The registry identifies it as a submission on behalf of PLFN on 8 May 2024.

### 4.1 Authorship distinction

The technical review is authored by EXP Services Inc. and was submitted by PLFN. It is therefore accurate to call it “the EXP technical review submitted by PLFN” or “PLFN's submitted EXP review.” Do not call it a PLFN-authored expert report unless separate evidence establishes that authorship.

The document is a comment-and-response table comparing EIS statements with reviewer questions, objections and requests for clarification. It is not a new site investigation and does not independently generate all underlying data quoted in its context columns.

### 4.2 Main issue groups extracted from the 15-page review

**A. Temporary waste relocation and water management**

The review questions where existing containment-cell waste would be temporarily moved before cell modification, whether the aeration stabilization basin or settling basins would be used, how the waste would be handled, what its composition was, and what controls would prevent impacts to water. It also questions the use of natural attenuation for Geotube dewatering effluent and asks what happens if treatment/attenuation is ineffective.

**B. Containment-cell design and long-term performance**

The review questions the completeness of the design, liner configuration, slope stability, potential vertical expansion, long-term cover and open faces, passive gas vents and odours, leachate collection, underdrain routing, and whether the cell's waste could be in contact with groundwater. It asks whether long-term monitoring includes pore water, slope stability and overall cell condition and what happens after the proposed post-closure monitoring horizon.

**C. Groundwater and potable-use assumptions**

The review raises concern that human-health and sediment target levels may rely on non-potable groundwater assumptions despite the nearby PLFN wellfield and the absence of municipal servicing. It asks whether the relevant groundwater should be treated as potable and whether the wellfield pathway has been adequately assessed.

**D. Existing groundwater data gaps**

The review states that a 2003 groundwater exploration report was missing figures and appendices from the reference set and that a 2010 groundwater monitoring report was nearly 11 years old at the time of review. It asks whether more current wellfield monitoring exists.

A quoted passage from the 2010 report states that calculated hydraulic head at MW-6 was below sea level and levels in OW-4 and OW-6 continued to fall, and that sustained below-sea-level head could allow Boat Harbour and/or Northumberland Strait water to affect wellfield quality. The EXP review asks that this warning be reconciled with EIS statements that no such relationship exists.

**Evidence limitation:** the underlying 2003 and 2010 reports have not been retrieved and re-examined in this review. The hydraulic-head passage is a quotation reproduced within the EXP review, not a new measurement by EXP or PLFN in 2021/2024. Obtain the complete underlying reports, figures, appendices, well logs, elevations, screen intervals and later monitoring records.

**E. Historical analyte coverage**

The review quotes a 2019 monitoring report as stating that VOC and PAH analysis was discontinued in 2003 and no further dioxin/furan analyses had been conducted. It asks whether this is a concern given dioxins/furans in the sediments intended for dredging.

It also identifies a Data Gap Assessment reference to 15 test pits with elevated metals, PAHs and dioxins/furans in the existing containment cell, but says the cited “Sampling and Analysis of Dredge Spoils Report” was not included in the reference documents. It requests the complete report.

**Evidence limitation:** obtain the 2019 monitoring report and the full dredge-spoils characterization report. Do not treat the quoted 2003 analytical-history statement or test-pit results as current site conditions.

**F. Wetland and habitat restoration**

The review questions the shift from ex-situ wetland remediation in an earlier decision document to natural attenuation in the EIS, and asks about the short post-remediation monitoring period. It also records concerns about channel depth, fish passage, and the difference between depth predicted by modelling and no proposed subsurface grading.

**G. Alternatives assessment**

The review questions whether off-site disposal and incineration were meaningfully assessed or removed from consideration early. These questions must be compared with the full remedial options decision document, provincial IR82 response, BGC external technical review and the actual criteria used to eliminate alternatives.

### 4.3 Review interpretation

The EXP review is particularly useful as a **structured issue register**. It identifies questions that should be converted into specific document requests, data checks and field-monitoring tests. It does not prove that each suspected pathway exists, that a design failure occurred, or that the proponent's answers were necessarily inadequate.

For each comment, the next database step is to create a row in the issue/claim ledger with:
- the exact EXP comment number and EIS page/reference;
- the exact underlying EIS statement;
- the evidence EXP cites;
- the question or requested clarification;
- the proponent/regulator response;
- whether the response was accepted, rejected, partially addressed or not located;
- the original source documents needed to test the issue; and
- the implications for independent monitoring if the issue remains unresolved.

## 11. Follow-up examination — Hive Engineering IR82 analysis, 4 August 2023

**New source located and reviewed:** Hive Engineering Limited, *Boat Harbour Remediation Environmental Impact Assessment — Information Requirement (IR) 82 Analysis*, project NS23.03.157, dated 4 August 2023.

**Official IAAC-hosted PDF:**  
https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80164/155835E.pdf

**Authorship/retainer:** The report states that Hive was retained by McKiggan Hebert Lawyers on behalf of PLFN to provide technical advice and analysis on the environmental assessment and the alternatives to the proposed permanent containment-cell location. It is therefore a consultant report prepared for PLFN, not a regulator's report and not an independent government study.

The complete PDF text, including Appendices A, B and C, has been reviewed through the official IAAC-hosted PDF. The original binary has not been acquired and hashed in the controlled evidence vault. It is class B under Document 12.

### 11.1 What Hive actually evaluated

The report compares four location categories:

1. Existing Boat Harbour containment cell.
2. Northern Pulp Nova Scotia pulp plant **active waste cell (Cell 3)**.
3. Mount William parcels.
4. Granton parcels.

This is more precise than saying Hive assessed the entire former Northern Pulp mill property. The report says NPNS had three landfill cells: Cells 1 and 2 closed, Cell 3 active under an existing Nova Scotia Environment and Climate Change approval. Hive reported no siting-criteria exceedances for the active cell and called it a viable candidate, while expressly excluding political and financial considerations from its analysis.

**Do not state that the whole former mill property, every Northern Pulp landfill cell, or the entire former mill site was proven suitable.** The report's positive comparison is specifically about the NPNS active waste cell (Cell 3) under the screening criteria it applied.

### 11.2 The scope and limitations are explicit in the report

Hive states in its closing section that:

- only siting requirements for a long-term hazardous-waste containment cell were considered;
- no field visits or studies were completed;
- publicly available data and data submitted by others for the Boat Harbour EIS were used;
- construction, operation, monitoring, closure and other requirements were assumed to be examined during later design and permitting.

This is a decisive limitation. The report is a desktop siting analysis, not a geotechnical investigation, hydrogeological study, environmental site assessment, detailed engineering design, waste-acceptance assessment, regulatory approval or proof that a particular site can accept the estimated waste volume.

### 11.3 Crown-land screening

Hive describes a GIS screening process within a 50-km radius:

- An initial size screen identified 1,376 Crown-land locations.
- After removing locations below the assumed minimum cell footprint, 928 locations remained potentially available.
- After screening for mapped streams, rivers, lakes, wetlands, old-growth forest, protected areas, significant species/habitat and potable-water wells, 109 Crown parcels remained.
- Three of those were within 10 km; the report gives distance bands for the remaining parcels.
- Appendix A lists the 109 screened parcels and surficial geology/overburden information.

These are **desktop-screened candidates**, not 109 sites confirmed suitable for a hazardous-waste facility. Mapped datasets, ownership, legal availability, site access, subsurface conditions, groundwater, geotechnical suitability, ecological impacts and community effects all require independent verification.

### 11.4 Private-land screening is expressly incomplete

Hive states that the private-land assessment relied on visual screening in the Service Nova Scotia Property Online web application because the application did not provide downloadable spatial data for GIS analysis. The report explicitly says the list is not comprehensive and that GeoNova files would need to be purchased to conduct a complete GIS screening. It also says depth to groundwater could only be assumed at this stage and requires detailed investigation.

Hive lists 97 private parcels, including 12 within 10 km. Appendix B contains owner names, property identifiers and location details. Those personal/property details are not reproduced in this public repository. The source can be reviewed in the restricted evidence environment and disclosed only under an approved privacy and legal process.

**Interpretation:** the 97-parcel list is a preliminary candidate list generated by a non-comprehensive visual screen. It is not an exhaustive land inventory and does not establish the availability or suitability of any listed property.

### 11.5 Siting criteria are a mixed-source screening framework

Hive says its analysis used criteria from:

- Nova Scotia Environment and Climate Change Municipal Solid Waste Landfill Guidelines;
- Canadian Council of Ministers of the Environment, *National Guidelines for Hazardous Waste Landfills* (PN 1365); and
- British Columbia Environmental Management Act / Hazardous Waste Regulation requirements.

The report distinguishes the source frameworks and applies them comparatively. Some criteria are from British Columbia regulations, not Nova Scotia legislation. The fact that a criterion is used in Hive's comparison does not, without further legal analysis, establish that it is a binding statutory siting rule in Nova Scotia.

For a defensible legal/technical comparison, the database must:
- obtain the exact versions of all three source documents used in 2023;
- verify whether each criterion was legally binding, incorporated by approval, guidance-only or used as a comparative benchmark;
- check subsequent amendments and current Nova Scotia approvals;
- reproduce the criterion and the source jurisdiction accurately; and
- separate a guideline exceedance from a legal violation or a demonstrated environmental failure.

### 11.6 Main site-comparison findings, accurately attributed

**Existing Boat Harbour cell:** Hive identifies exceedances against several criteria, including groundwater separation, proximity to Boat Harbour/wetlands, property setback, surface-water isolation, special-significance/wildlife-area isolation, groundwater isolation, 3-metre unsaturated-soil separation, 300-metre waterbody separation and potable-water-supply/recharge-area criteria. Hive states that groundwater is in contact with the lowest point of the existing cell, based on test-pit logs and monitoring wells. That underlying evidence must be obtained and checked.

**NPNS active waste cell (Cell 3):** Hive reports no exceedances against the criteria it applied and describes the active cell as a viable candidate, but says political and financial considerations were outside scope. The report does not establish available capacity for the Boat Harbour waste volume, waste acceptance, operational compatibility, long-term liability, community consent, ownership arrangements, or regulatory approval to receive this material.

**Mount William:** Hive describes the four parcels and considers the site marginally viable, with several siting criteria exceedances that it believes could be mitigated. These include proximity to wetlands/watercourses, buildings and populated/public areas. Some mitigation suggestions include stream realignment, property buffers and engineering measures. No field studies were done.

**Granton:** Hive describes the site as somewhat viable, with a small number of setback exceedances that it believes may be mitigated, but notes size/shape limitations and potential need to acquire neighbouring property to create adequate buffers. No field studies were done.

### 11.7 Groundwater/wellfield inference

Hive states that the PLFN off-peninsula wellfield is approximately 2 km from the existing containment cell. It cites the 2016 AECOM hydrogeology assessment and 2010 Dillon groundwater monitoring report, including concerns about pumping water levels falling below sea level and a possible reversal of the predicted horizontal flow direction.

Hive then states that, for the purpose of its analysis, it is reasonable to conclude that the existing Boat Harbour containment cell is within the recharge area for the PLFN wellfield.

This is a reasoned inference made by Hive from the reports it cites. It is not a new field measurement or a new calibrated groundwater-flow model in the Hive report. It must be tested by obtaining the complete 2010 and 2016 source reports, well construction details, water-level time series, pumping rates, elevations, hydraulic testing and any later monitoring. Do not turn this inference into an established hydraulic connection without that work.

### 11.8 Internal consistency / parcel identity checks

The Hive report itself contains parcel-description details that need reconciliation before using the Mount William analysis as a precise land record:

- Its opening description identifies a 29.14-hectare PLFN parcel as a potential alternative.
- Its site-comparison section describes a four-parcel Mount William area and identifies PID 00865469 as 29.14 hectares, while also listing PID 00801241 as another large parcel.
- The four PIDs named in the Mount William section are 00865485, 00865469, 65170508 and 65170516; the mention of PID 00801241 in the same section needs to be reconciled against the original parcel maps, title records and report table.
- The report's Appendix A and Appendix C contain parcel IDs, screening assumptions and site comparisons that must be checked against current authoritative cadastral and well databases.

No property should be mapped or treated as PLFN-owned, available or selected solely from this report. Obtain current official parcel maps, ownership/title records and PLFN confirmation.

### 11.9 How this changes the earlier PLFN source analysis

The Final Position's headline numbers (109 Crown and 97 private parcels) are traceable to the Hive report. However, the full Hive report narrows what those numbers mean:

- 109 Crown parcels are the result of a GIS desktop screen using mapped exclusions and a 50-km radius.
- 97 private parcels are the result of a non-comprehensive visual screen, and Hive expressly says a complete GIS screen would require GeoNova data.
- Groundwater depth for private parcels was assumed and requires detailed investigation.
- The four comparative sites were not subjected to field investigations in this report.
- The NPNS finding concerns its active waste cell, Cell 3, and excludes political/financial factors.
- The report does not establish that any alternative is fully permitted, available, accepted by PLFN, technically designed for the specific waste, or ready to receive it.

This strengthens the record of PLFN having commissioned a technical alternative-site screen, while requiring more careful language about what that screen proves.
