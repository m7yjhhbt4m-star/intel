# Boat Harbour / A’se’k — Registry Records Map and Completeness Audit

**Version:** 1.0 — 22 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Correct omissions in the earlier research package, map the primary federal assessment record set, distinguish documents located from documents actually reviewed, and prevent the repository from presenting a partial review as a complete record recovery.  
**Status:** Desk-research audit. The registry contains substantially more material than has been individually extracted and analysed. This document is an index and gap-control record, not a claim that all 152 registry entries or every appendix have been read.

## 1. Why this document was necessary

The previous package had incorporated important findings from the Environmental Assessment Report, the final Decision Statement, EIS Volume IV, selected technical studies and selected information-request responses. It did **not** make the full underlying federal record set sufficiently visible in the README and source register. It also did not clearly distinguish the 81 consolidated Round 1 responses from the later response to IR82.

That was an unacceptable completeness problem. The source material was not all absent, but the repository did not clearly expose the full record inventory, its status, or the material that still required review.

This audit makes the following distinctions:

- **Located:** a public registry entry or direct source has been identified.
- **Opened/reviewed:** the relevant document content has actually been examined.
- **Partially reviewed:** selected sections, tables, or passages have been extracted; the whole document has not been systematically analysed.
- **Not yet reviewed:** the document is known and linked, but its contents have not been examined for this project.
- **Underlying data unavailable:** a report describes results but the raw laboratory files, certificates, GIS, time series, or model files are not supplied in the accessible document.

A link in this register does not mean the underlying document has been fully reviewed.

## 3. Information Requirements — correction to the earlier account

The registry record establishes a more complete sequence than the earlier summary made clear:

1. IAAC issued **82 Round 1 Information Requirements** across Parts 1–4.
2. The proponent consolidated responses to **IRs 1–81** in the 5 August 2022 consolidated report (registry document reference 40).
3. The response to **IR82** was submitted separately in February 2024 (registry document reference 43), with three linked items:
   - Build Nova Scotia response to IR82 (approximately 40.1 MB);
   - Technical and Economic Feasibility Assessment for IR82 (approximately 2.2 MB);
   - Hive Engineering Report — supplemental information provided by Pictou Landing First Nation (approximately 1.5 MB).
4. The registry also lists Round 2 Part 1 information requests and response material in July 2022, including the agency's technical-review outcome and the proponent response.

This means the accurate statement is **not** “81 of 82 remained answered with one outstanding.” The 2022 consolidated package covered IRs 1–81; IR82 received a separate response in 2024. ### 4.4 Public registry comments and attachments

The registry search page returned **152 entries: 81 documents and 71 comments** at the time checked. It is therefore incorrect to treat the key-documents list as the entire public record.

One specific comment surfaced in the registry: reference 68, a 28 November 2024 submission titled “Other Options Not Considered that could improve overall sludge amounts, dewatering and timelines,” from Buckman Laboratories of Canada Ltd. It includes a proposed biological/bioremediation and biological-dredging approach and supporting promotional/technical attachments.

This is a **proposal/lead**, not verified Boat Harbour field evidence, not proof of performance on Boat Harbour sediment, and not an approved remediation method. It should be screened as an alternative-treatment claim: identify the exact organisms/agents and mechanism, target contaminants, mass balance, by-products, ecotoxicity, pilot data, scale-up evidence, treatment residuals, and independent peer-reviewed validation. Do not assume that “bioremediation” destroys metals; metals are elements and can only be transformed in speciation/mobility or transferred/immobilized, not destroyed.

Registry search page and entry:
https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/evaluations/exploration?culture=en-CA&projDocs=80164

**Important limitation:** This audit has not yet systematically read all 71 public comments and attachments. The full comment set remains an explicit research task. The contributions page reports 71 results and is paginated; only the displayed first-page material and selected key attachments have been screened in the latest pass. The 152-entry search result count is a registry snapshot and may change.

## 6. Updated priority order — close omissions before new sensor procurement

1. Build an 82-row Round 1 information-requirement concordance: IR question → proponent response → page/table/appendix → regulator/technical reviewer comment → final condition → unresolved issue.
2. Systematically review EIS Volumes I–V, beginning with Volume IV and the relevant technical chapters in the other volumes.
3. Extract Appendix Z (groundwater/surface water), Appendix Y (geology/geochemistry), Appendix A (HHERA), Appendix AA (wetlands/terrestrial) and Appendix BB (aquatic environments), including embedded consultant reports and appendices.
4. Obtain and audit the complete GHD 580-page pilot report and underlying process/lab data.
5. Obtain full Phase II/Supplemental Phase II and groundwater datasets, including well construction and hydrogeological records.
6. Review the 2025 Annual Report and all subsequent registry updates; obtain final follow-up programmes and provincial approvals.
7. Recover complete marine, sediment and biota datasets and coordinates.
8. Reconstruct the 2014 spill footprint and recover the original investigation and follow-up record.

### P1 — Complete overlooked context and alternatives

9. Screen all 71 registry comments and attachments, tagging technical evidence, claims, requests, rights-holder submissions and unverified proposals.
10. Review the full BGC external technical review and its plain-language summary against the 82 IR responses and final project design.
11. Under PLFN direction, review consultation, well-being and ecological-knowledge material for monitoring priorities and governance requirements.
12. Review the full environmental management plan and all monitoring/contingency procedures; distinguish draft, proposed, approved and implemented versions.
13. Evaluate the biological-dredging/bioremediation proposal in registry comment 68 as an unverified alternative, not as a proven technology.

### P2 — Design only after evidence recovery

14. Finalize sampling stations and seasonal coverage only after GIS reconstruction, hydrographic review, and PLFN direction.
15. Calculate statistical power and detection capability using recovered variance, detection limits and expected effect sizes.
16. Compare drones, hyperspectral sensors, sondes, ADCPs, sediment traps, passive samplers, sonar and autonomous platforms against defined measurement decisions.
17. Establish public reporting, exceedance triggers, data custody, chain-of-custody and independent review before fieldwork.

## 9. Bottom line

The earlier package contained important findings but did not make the full evidence universe and its incomplete review status sufficiently visible. This update corrects that.

The most important newly exposed work is not buying another sensor. It is systematically extracting the EIS appendices, auditing all 82 information requirements, screening the 71 public comments, and recovering the original datasets and operating records. Until those tasks are complete, the research package must be described as a substantial but incomplete desk review—not a complete independent environmental evidence review.
