# Boat Harbour / A’se’k — Registry Records Map and Completeness Audit

**Version:** 1.0 — 22 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Correct omissions in the earlier research package, map the primary federal assessment record set, distinguish documents located from documents actually reviewed, and prevent the repository from presenting a partial review as a complete record recovery.  
**Status:** Desk-research audit. The registry contains substantially more material than has been individually extracted and analysed. This document is an index and gap-control record, not a claim that all 152 registry entries or every appendix have been read.

## 1. Why this document was necessary

The previous package had incorporated important findings from the Environmental Assessment Report, the final Decision Statement, EIS Volume IV, selected technical studies and selected information-request responses. It did **not** make the full underlying federal record set sufficiently visible in the README and source register. It also did not clearly distinguish the 81 consolidated Round 1 responses from the later response to IR82.

That was an unacceptable completeness problem. The source material was not all absent, but the repository did not clearly expose the full record inventory, its status, or the material that still required review.

This audit makes the following distinctions:

- **Located:** a public registry entry or direct source has been identified.
- **Opened/reviewed:** the relevant document content has actually been examined.
- **Partially reviewed:** selected sections, tables, or passages have been extracted; the whole document has not been systematically analysed.
- **Not yet reviewed:** the document is known and linked, but its contents have not been examined for this project.
- **Underlying data unavailable:** a report describes results but the raw laboratory files, certificates, GIS, time series, or model files are not supplied in the accessible document.

A link in this register does not mean the underlying document has been fully reviewed.

### 4.4 Public registry comments and attachments

The registry search page returned **152 entries: 81 documents and 71 comments** at the time checked. It is therefore incorrect to treat the key-documents list as the entire public record.

One specific comment surfaced in the registry: reference 68, a 28 November 2024 submission titled “Other Options Not Considered that could improve overall sludge amounts, dewatering and timelines,” from Buckman Laboratories of Canada Ltd. It includes a proposed biological/bioremediation and biological-dredging approach and supporting promotional/technical attachments.

This is a **proposal/lead**, not verified Boat Harbour field evidence, not proof of performance on Boat Harbour sediment, and not an approved remediation method. It should be screened as an alternative-treatment claim: identify the exact organisms/agents and mechanism, target contaminants, mass balance, by-products, ecotoxicity, pilot data, scale-up evidence, treatment residuals, and independent peer-reviewed validation. Do not assume that “bioremediation” destroys metals; metals are elements and can only be transformed in speciation/mobility or transferred/immobilized, not destroyed.

## 12. IAAC Appendix B — document 137872E substantive review update — 25 November 2026

- **Located:** Yes — official IAAC registry PDF, 17 MB.
- **Opened:** Yes — complete extracted text is accessible (6,526 lines).
- **Review status:** Substantive partial review. The EMP/PEPP structure, document status, governance and monitoring responsibilities, groundwater, surface water, effluent/leachate, turbidity/TSS, marine/fish monitoring, follow-up and attached ancestral-remains protocol markings were examined. This is not a claim that every page, drawing, table or attachment has been reviewed line by line.
- **Document composition:** The single registry PDF bundles (1) Nova Scotia Lands Inc./GHD Environmental Management Plan, revision 1.0 dated 30 September 2020, and (2) GHD Project Environmental Protection Plan, revision 1.0 dated 1 October 2020, plus forms and attachments. Both principal plans are expressly DRAFT; the PEPP says it was prepared while the EIS was under review and permits/approvals were still in progress. They are not proof of final approved procedures or implementation.
- **Repository record:** Source register entry BH-115 added to `05-source-register-and-evidence-quality.md`.

### Monitoring and evidence implications

1. **Groundwater:** The draft describes an existing monitoring-well network and weekly groundwater-quality sampling during remediation; it calls for annual detailed interpretation of long-term results. The PDF does not itself inventory the wells, coordinates, screened intervals, historical measurements or complete final sampling protocol.
2. **Surface water:** The draft proposes seasonal wet/dry-event sampling at the main streams entering Boat Harbour and the containment-cell pond outfall. Wet events are defined as rainfall above 5 mm; dry events follow 3–4 days without rain. This is not a complete Strait-wide baseline or independent monitoring design.
3. **Dredging/dewatering effluent:** Daily 24-hour composite samples, CALA-accredited laboratory analysis, up-to-48-hour expedited results and calculated daily mass loads are proposed. The mass-loading tables include zero limits and dash entries without numeric limits; their operational meaning, detection limits and final permit status require verification.
4. **TSS/turbidity:** The PEPP proposes TSS monitoring multiple times per day and a 25 mg/L-above-background criterion outside active dredging areas, alongside separate NTU-based monitoring/action points. The action point is >4 NTU above background (approximately 100 m downstream of a cofferdam) and requires in-water work to stop; the monitoring-point table separately refers to a daily average >8 NTU above background for 24 hours. These are distinct proposed measures/locations and must not be collapsed into one threshold.
5. **Marine environment:** The draft says remediation-stage marine monitoring is anticipated to consist primarily of regular observation/documentation. It defers the detailed program and post-remediation parameters to later consultation/HHERA/regulator work; proposed post-remediation parameters include water quality, sediment composition/quality, benthic communities and contaminants in biota. This draft alone does not demonstrate a complete marine/Strait monitoring program.
6. **Leachate:** The temporary treatment capacity and post-closure flow figures are design expectations, not measured performance. They require comparison with final design, permits, operating records and actual annual monitoring.
7. **Monitoring accountability:** The draft allocates contractor construction monitoring, CMOC compliance/follow-up monitoring and Proponent/designee long-term monitoring. Its Independent Monitor is described as providing an audit function/advice to NSLI and CMOC. This is distinct from the later binding 2025 Decision Statement condition requiring a third-party independent environmental monitor to report to the Proponent, Mi’kmaq of Nova Scotia and IAAC, and the separate Indigenous-monitor requirement.

### Later controlling document cross-check

Conditions 3.4–3.7 require sediment-transport modelling before dam removal and follow-up monitoring of benthic habitat, surface-water quality/TSS and sediment deposition in the estuary and Northumberland Strait embayment through post-closure. ### High-value findings for the independent monitoring investigation

1. **Geographic boundary:** The EIS states the study area is about 546 ha and includes an adjacent marine environment in the Northumberland Strait. The estuary itself is described as 7.6 ha, with about 4 ha impacted and 27,000 m³ of sludge/sediment. The EIS scope is not the entire Northumberland Strait.
2. **Waste-volume reconciliation:** The EIS reports about 1.39 million m³ of unconsolidated contaminated sludge/sediment and 770,000–922,400 m³ after consolidation/dewatering. It separately gives 311,000 m³ for wetland unconsolidated material in one summary and 352,000 m³ of wetland sludge/sediment/root mass in the project description. These figures use different bases and need reconciliation against the underlying survey and volume calculations.
3. **Containment cell:** The 2020 EIS proposes expanding the existing 6.7 ha cell from its 220,000 m³ approved capacity to a total of 1,073,000 m³, including a 143,000 m³ contingency above the 930,000 m³ base design. The proposed design basis includes a 100-year leachate collection system and 150-year geomembrane, using Ontario Regulation 232/98 as a design reference because the cited Nova Scotia guideline lacked service-life requirements and CCME standards were not applied to the existing cell. These are design claims, not demonstrated service life.
4. **Dioxins/furans and leachate:** The EIS treats dioxins/furans as unlikely to mobilize into the liquid phase because of hydrophobicity/low solubility; lead and zinc were carried forward as service-life contaminants of concern. The forecast leachate values and chemical assumptions require validation against measured leachate and groundwater data.
5. **Dredging:** The selected EIS method was wet hydraulic dredging with Geotube dewatering, at an estimated 4,000 m³/day. The report recognizes reduced visual confirmation during wet dredging and proposes confirmatory sampling and clean-up passes. GPS-based tracking is described as necessary to meet target cuts and avoid excessive removal.
6. **Wetlands:** The EIS proposes natural attenuation for areas below risk criteria and ex-situ removal for hotspots/areas above criteria. It reports 258,000 m³ impacted sludge and 71,000 m³ sediment needing additional risk management. Natural attenuation is ranked preferred overall in the comparison, while ex-situ remediation is ranked preferred on social/community-acceptance criteria. The selected approach is therefore mixed and should not be described as removal of all impacted wetland material.
7. **Bulk water:** The EIS relies on natural attenuation and large groundwater/surface-water inflows to the BHSL (7,200 m³/day low, 64,000 m³/day peak, 28,000 m³/day average). It reports improvement at Point C in summer 2020 compared with 2018–2019 samples but says additional contributing-stream sampling was still planned. This limited comparison does not establish whole-harbour, estuary or Strait-wide recovery.
8. **Dewatering effluent:** The EIS estimates 5.2 million m³ of Geotube effluent during dredging. The proposed sequence is chemical dosing/Geotube filtration, return to the BHSL or an unremediated area, and management through natural attenuation. The 5.2 million m³ effluent figure and the separate 5.7 million m³ bulk/dewatering-water estimate require water-balance reconciliation; do not add them or treat them as the same stream.
9. **Leachate:** The 2020 EIS describes temporary on-site treatment after dredging (average <200 m³/day, peak 1,000 m³/day) with compliant treated effluent returned to the estuary discharge point and off-spec water recirculated; long-term leachate would be trucked off-site. Post-closure generation is estimated below 2,500 m³/year, decreasing over time, with a 40 m³ tank and 10–40 m³ tankers. These are forecasts, not measured performance.
10. **Alternative-screening transparency:** Thermal destruction/separation was excluded after vendor bench testing reviewed by GHD under an NDA; the EIS says the technology was unproven and performance risk could not be reasonably mitigated. The underlying vendor results are not presented transparently in this EIS. A new cell was screened out partly on stakeholder acceptability/location grounds; off-site disposal was disadvantaged by the absence of an approved receiving landfill and the projected 18,200 truckloads.
