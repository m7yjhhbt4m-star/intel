## 1. Purpose and scope

This document compares the principal records concerning Information Requirement 82 (IR82) and alternative long-term containment-cell locations:

1. IAAC's IR82 requirement, reproduced in the July 2022 assessment.
2. Build Nova Scotia / Nova Scotia Lands Inc. (NSLands), *Technical and Economic Feasibility Assessment — Alternative Containment Cell Location Site*, July 2022, prepared by GHD and including a Nova Scotia Environment and Climate Change (NSECC) memorandum and GHD annexes.
3. Hive Engineering Limited, *IR82 Analysis*, 4 August 2023, retained by McKiggan Hebert Lawyers for PLFN.
4. PLFN's 24 August 2023 response to the provincial feasibility assessment, reproduced as Annex 1 in Build Nova Scotia's February 2024 response and included in PLFN's August 2023 Document Brief.
5. Build Nova Scotia, *Response to Information Requirement 82 and Address of PLFN Feedback*, 6 February 2024, including Annex 2 provincial replies and Annex 3 supporting documents.
6. PLFN's 23 April 2024 Final Position.

This is an evidence comparison, not a finding that either side's legal or technical conclusions are correct. The reports have different purposes, scopes, assumptions and evidentiary bases. They should not be treated as interchangeable.

## 3. What IAAC actually asked under IR82

The July 2022 technical assessment reproduces IR82. IAAC requested an analysis of the technical and economic feasibility of the alternative containment-cell location proposed by PLFN. It specified factors including environmental impacts, cost, regulatory requirements, timing, risk, public concerns and impacts to PLFN. It also required sufficient information to support assumptions and conclusions, and required PLFN be given an opportunity to comment with a clear demonstration of how those comments were addressed.

The proponent and PLFN disagree about the scope and implications of this requirement:

- **Build Nova Scotia's interpretation:** IR82 required a technical/economic assessment of the specific Mount William parcel proposed by PLFN. It did not require a broad search of all other potential sites. The February 2024 response repeatedly says the other 109 Crown and 97 private parcels are outside IR82 and that off-site disposal had already been assessed generically in the EIS/Remedial Options Decision Document.
- **PLFN's position:** Mount William was an example intended to demonstrate that alternatives could be found; PLFN did not intend to limit consideration to one site. PLFN says other sites, including the former Northern Pulp landfill, should have been considered.
- **Hive's work:** expands the desktop screen beyond the Mount William parcel by identifying Crown/private candidate parcels and comparing four site categories.

**Finding:** The record establishes a genuine disagreement over the scope of IR82 and the adequacy of the broader alternatives process. It does not, by itself, decide whether the proponent complied with IR82 or whether the Crown's consultation/accommodation duties were fulfilled. That is a legal question for counsel and the relevant decision record.

## 4. The site footprints are not identical

### 4.1 Provincial 2022 assessment

The GHD/NSLands assessment states that it compared the existing Boat Harbour containment cell with the PLFN-proposed site comprising PIDs 00865469 and adjacent PID 00865485. NSECC's memorandum describes the two properties as approximately 59 hectares in total.

### 4.2 Hive 2023 analysis

Hive's report includes:
- the existing Boat Harbour containment cell;
- NPNS active waste Cell 3;
- a four-parcel Mount William site; and
- Granton parcels.

The four PIDs identified in Hive's Mount William section are 00865485, 00865469, 65170508 and 65170516. Other parcel references appear in the report and require reconciliation against authoritative cadastral records.

### 4.3 Consequence

The Province's 2022 site-specific assessment and Hive's 2023 comparative analysis do not appear to assess exactly the same land footprint. Any claim that one report disproves the other must first reconcile the parcel boundaries, ownership, area, proposed cell footprint, access and environmental study boundaries.

**Required record:** authoritative parcel maps, current title/ownership, surveys, PLFN confirmation of the intended parcel group, and GIS files used by each consultant. Do not map or publish private owners' personal details in the public repository.

## 5. Siting criteria: different legal and technical questions

### 5.1 What NSECC said in its 2022 memorandum

The NSECC memorandum states that Nova Scotia did not then have guidelines specific to hazardous-waste landfill siting. It describes a hierarchy for looking to other jurisdictions where provincial-specific standards are absent, and says the Nova Scotia Municipal Solid Waste Landfill Guidelines would be used as minimum guidance. It also refers to federal, Ontario and British Columbia material.

The memorandum discusses:
- the CCME National Guidelines for Hazardous Waste Landfills;
- Nova Scotia Municipal Solid Waste Landfill Guidelines; and
- British Columbia Environmental Management Act / Hazardous Waste Regulation criteria.

### 5.2 What Hive did

Hive used the criteria identified through the provincial assessment as a comparative framework and applied them to the existing Boat Harbour cell, NPNS active Cell 3, Mount William and Granton. Hive reported multiple criteria exceedances for the existing Boat Harbour cell, four for Mount William, and none for NPNS active Cell 3 under the criteria it applied.

### 5.3 Legal and evidentiary distinction

A criterion appearing in a consultant's comparison does not automatically mean:
- it is a binding Nova Scotia statutory requirement;
- it applies retrospectively to an existing facility;
- it is incorporated into the current Industrial Approval;
- an exceedance is a proven legal violation; or
- a site that has no mapped exceedance is approved or technically suitable.

The Province's stated position is that current-day siting criteria are not used to force an existing facility to relocate, although they may inform mitigation and footprint limitations. It says the Mount William site was assessed against current criteria because it was a proposed new greenfield facility.

**Unresolved legal/technical question:** which criteria govern the proposed vertical expansion or modification of the existing containment cell, under the existing approval and any amended approval? This requires the full Industrial Approval, amendments, current statutes/regulations, regulator decision records and legal advice.

## 6. Environmental and field-investigation limits

### 6.1 Mount William: provincial assessment

The Province's technical assessment states that site-specific wetland surveys were not conducted. NSECC's memorandum states that an ecological, hydrological and hydrogeological assessment had not been completed for the site(s), and that it relied on mapped provincial wetland data, topographic maps, photographs and satellite imagery to make its observations.

The assessment identifies mapped wetlands, watercourses, nearby buildings, groundwater concerns and the Middle River watershed. These are material screening issues, but mapped presence and desktop interpretation are not substitutes for site-specific field delineation, seasonal high-water measurements, groundwater monitoring, hydraulic testing, species surveys or detailed engineering.

### 6.2 Mount William and other candidates: Hive assessment

Hive expressly states that it completed no field visits or field studies. It relies on publicly available information and EIS data. It says private parcel screening is not comprehensive, because it used visual screening of Property Online rather than full downloadable GIS data; GeoNova files would be needed for a complete screen. It also says groundwater depth assumptions require detailed investigation.

### 6.3 Consequence

Neither desktop assessment establishes final site suitability. A proper independent comparison would require, for each candidate:
- field wetland delineation over appropriate seasons;
- hydrology and watershed delineation;
- hydrogeological investigation, monitoring wells, seasonal high-water data and hydraulic testing;
- geotechnical and seismic investigation;
- surface-water and groundwater baseline sampling;
- ecological/species-at-risk surveys;
- archaeological and cultural-resource review under appropriate rights-holder protocols;
- land title, access, zoning, ownership and legal availability;
- waste acceptance, design capacity, leachate treatment, closure and post-closure obligations; and
- consultation and rights-holder governance.

## 7. The NPNS active Cell 3 finding is narrow

Hive's report says NPNS had three landfill cells, Cells 1 and 2 closed and Cell 3 active under an existing NSECC approval. It reports no siting-criteria exceedances for the active Cell 3 and calls it a viable candidate under the criteria used, while expressly excluding political and financial considerations.

This is not a finding that:
- the entire former Northern Pulp mill site is suitable;
- Cells 1 and 2 are suitable;
- Cell 3 has sufficient capacity for Boat Harbour waste;
- its approval permits this waste stream;
- its owner will accept the material;
- its design, liner, leachate, groundwater and post-closure systems are suitable for the proposed material;
- the site has no environmental liabilities; or
- the site has been selected by the Province or PLFN.

Build Nova Scotia's February 2024 response says the Cell 3 discussion is irrelevant to IR82. The response does not provide a substantive site-specific technical assessment of Cell 3 in the passages reviewed.

**Next work:** retrieve the current Cell 3 approval and all amendments, waste acceptance criteria, capacity, monitoring data, groundwater/surface-water data, leachate records, closure obligations, site ownership and the owner's position. The 2026 alternative-site discussions must be checked against these records; do not infer selection from a statement that the site is being considered.

## 8. Groundwater dispute: Hive inference versus provincial rebuttal

Hive quotes the 2016 AECOM Boat Harbour Hydrogeology Assessment and the 2010 Dillon Groundwater Monitoring Program Report. It notes that the 2016 AECOM assessment considered the PLFN off-peninsula wellfield safe provided pumping water levels remained above sea level, and that below-sea-level pumping could potentially alter the predicted horizontal flow direction. Hive quotes the 2010 Dillon report's warning that sustained levels below sea level could change groundwater flow and draw Boat Harbour or saltwater toward the wellfield.

Hive then infers, for purposes of its analysis, that the existing containment cell is within the recharge area of the PLFN wellfield.

Build Nova Scotia's February 2024 response says Hive misinterpreted both reports and that they concluded there was no hydraulic connection between Boat Harbour/the containment cell and the drinking-water wells. It also says PLFN's own consultants concluded Boat Harbour posed no risk to the wellfield.

**Status:** disputed and unresolved. The Hive report is not a new groundwater field investigation; the proponent response is not a substitute for the underlying hydrogeological data. Neither party's summary should be adopted without examining the complete 2010 Dillon and 2016 AECOM reports and raw supporting data.

**Priority evidence request:**
- full 2010 Dillon report and all appendices;
- full 2016 AECOM report and all appendices;
- monitoring-well construction logs, screen intervals, elevations and coordinates;
- pumping-well logs, rates and operating schedules;
- raw water-level time series, including seasonal and pumping conditions;
- hydraulic testing, aquifer parameters and model files;
- model boundary conditions, calibration, sensitivity and uncertainty;
- post-2016 and current wellfield data; and
- source-to-receptor analysis for the containment cell, ASB, Boat Harbour and Northumberland Strait.

Do not state that the PLFN wellfield is currently contaminated or that a hydraulic connection has been proved. Equally, do not state that the pathway is definitively excluded until the underlying evidence and uncertainty are reviewed.

## 9. Cost estimate — what the $108 million figure means

The July 2022 GHD assessment presents a Class D estimate for the PLFN-proposed Mount William alternative of approximately $107.979 million. It reports a range of approximately $86.383 million to $161.987 million, corresponding to a stated accuracy of -20% to +50%.

The detailed GHD planning/cost memorandum states that:
- the estimate is in 2020 dollars;
- it excludes HST and the time value of money;
- supply-chain inflation impacts were not incorporated;
- no detailed quotations were obtained for most activities;
- the scope, schedule and costs rely on assumptions and judgments;
- additional scope and schedule may be required; and
- GHD does not warrant that the project could be delivered at the estimated cost.

The cost estimate includes property/zoning, environmental assessment and baseline studies, approvals, design/construction, operation and a 25-year post-closure care/maintenance allowance in the detailed GHD memo. That memo assumes no financial assurance is required and assumes leachate can be sent off site for treatment at up to 100 m³/year.

The main comparative report also says long-term maintenance and monitoring costs were excluded from the comparison because they were considered similar. The relationship between that statement and the detailed GHD cost schedule's 25-year post-closure allowance needs reconciliation. The database should not assume the two cost statements are directly comparable.

The report identifies approximately $21 million for improvements to the existing containment cell and treats the alternative-site cost as incremental, on the basis that the existing cell would still be required for sludge consolidation/dewatering. The exact common-cost and incremental-cost treatment should be independently reconstructed from the cost workbook and underlying quantities.

**Current status:** a conceptual Class D planning estimate, not a tender price, approved budget for a new site, current 2026 estimate or verified final cost.

## 10. Trucking and greenhouse-gas figures — material internal inconsistency

This review identified a material mismatch between the July 2022 report's main text and its own Annexes 6 and 7.

### 10.1 Trucking assumptions

The GHD Class C memorandum for disposal at the Stablex facility in Blainville, Quebec states:
- approximately 1,150,000 m³ of waste including 15% contingency;
- 18 m³ per truck, approximately 22.5 metric tonnes at an assumed density of 1.25 t/m³;
- approximately 63,000 loaded truckloads;
- 1,250 km one-way to Stablex;
- 70 truckloads/day, five days/week, 48 weeks/year, 3.8 years;
- a wide preliminary tipping-fee range of $225 to $3,400 per tonne;
- the cost estimate used the lower end of the tipping-fee range because waste characteristics were expected to fall within an acceptable range; and
- the memo excludes PLFN participation/engagement, an independent monitor, decommissioning/restoration of the existing containment cell and NSLI project management.

The Class C memo is expressly marked **DRAFT** and says its contents and opinions must not be relied upon. It reports an estimated $762.603 million with a stated range of approximately $610.083 million to $991.384 million. The report's main text refers to $763 million and a range of $610–991 million.

### 10.2 Greenhouse-gas Annex 7

The 7 April 2022 GHD GHG memorandum is also expressly marked **DRAFT** and says its contents, opinions, conclusions and recommendations must not be relied upon.

It separates two scenarios:

- **Scenario 1:** haul consolidated waste to an existing third-party licensed hazardous-waste facility approximately 1,250 km away. The memo estimates 183,164 tonnes CO₂e.
- **Scenario 2:** construct a new landfill approximately 20 km from the Boat Harbour project site. The memo estimates 21,530 tonnes CO₂e.

The Annex 7 comparison table associates 183,164 tonnes CO₂e with approximately 42,896 homes' annual energy consumption, and 21,530 tonnes CO₂e with approximately 5,042 homes.

### 10.3 Main-text attribution problem

The July 2022 assessment's main text and executive summary attribute 183,164 tonnes CO₂e to the **PLFN-proposed Mount William site scenario**, while comparing that number to approximately 5,042 homes. Annex 7 assigns 183,164 tonnes to the long-haul third-party facility scenario and assigns approximately 5,042 homes to the 21,530-tonne new-landfill scenario.

This is a direct internal mismatch in scenario attribution and equivalency. Build Nova Scotia's February 2024 response repeats the main-text figures. The database must not repeat the 183,164-tonne figure as the Mount William scenario without resolving the inconsistency.

### 10.4 Distance-basis problem

The main assessment describes the Mount William alternative as an approximate 20 km round trip. Annex 7 describes the new landfill as located 20 km from the project site and its calculation table uses 40 km under a “Distance (Round Trip)” heading. The Stablex memorandum describes 1,250 km one-way, while the GHG calculation table labels its distance column “Round Trip” and uses 1,250.

These distance descriptions and calculation-table headings need a formal reconciliation. Obtain the original calculation workbook, route assumptions, loaded/empty trip counts and fuel calculation sheets. Recalculate both scenarios independently.

### 10.5 Consequence

The GHG comparison is not ready for legal or technical reliance. It contains a clear scenario/equivalency mismatch in the published report text, both underlying GHD annex memoranda are marked draft/non-reliance, and the distance/trip-count basis requires reconciliation. The 315,020-tonne “net decrease” figure also comes from the earlier 2018 GHG assessment and must be checked against its baseline, time horizon, system boundary and updated project scope.

## 11. Consultation history and the 2019 land-use plan

### 11.1 Competing accounts of support/opposition

Build Nova Scotia's February 2024 response says the Province believed PLFN implicitly supported the overall project in 2019–2020, while acknowledging that opposition to the containment-cell element became formal/adamant in March 2021.

The same response's Annex 2 chronology records PLFN correspondence dated 30 May 2018 with comments on the Remedial Options Design Document, including opposition to use of the existing containment cell. The proponent says there was no consensus but distinguishes this from formal opposition.

PLFN's submissions describe opposition to the existing containment cell and argue that consultation was inadequate. The record therefore requires a precise distinction between:
- support for remediation of Boat Harbour as a goal;
- support for specific remediation methods;
- support or opposition to long-term storage in the existing cell;
- participation in meetings or planning; and
- consent to the project or to the permanent containment-cell option.

Do not infer consent to permanent storage from attendance, general support for remediation, a public statement about returning A’se’k, participation in a land-use planning process or receipt of project information.

### 11.2 2019 A’se’k Land Use Plan

Build Nova Scotia's response includes the February 2019 Membertou Geomatics Solutions A’se’k Land Use Plan in Annex 3. It identifies PLFN and Nova Scotia Lands as parties to the planning work, says the plan was developed through engagement with PLFN committees/community, and describes it as a community-driven plan.

However, the plan itself describes “formal adoption” as a stage in the planning model, says Chief and Council would make a later decision about amendments or whether to supersede existing by-laws with adoption of the new plan, and identifies the plan as a future land-use guide.

IAAC's January 2025 report records PLFN's statement that the proponent-funded land-use plan had not been ratified by PLFN membership or adopted through a formal Band Council Resolution.

**Accurate characterization:** a 2019 land-use plan was prepared by Membertou Geomatics Solutions through a process described as involving PLFN committees and community and supported/funded by the Province. Formal adoption by PLFN has not been established in the records reviewed. Do not call it an adopted PLFN land-use decision or proof of consent to the project.

## 12. What the February 2024 Build Nova Scotia response establishes — and what it does not

The February 2024 response is a proponent-authored composite:
- executive summary and cover letter from Build Nova Scotia;
- Annex 1 reproducing PLFN's August 2023 comments;
- Annex 2 giving the Province/Proponent's response to those comments; and
- Annex 3 with supporting documents, including the 2019 land-use plan, 2014 agreements and other records.

It is primary evidence of the proponent's February 2024 response and the Province's stated position. Its Annex 1 reproduces PLFN's comments but should not replace the original PLFN-authored submission when citing PLFN's own position. Its Annex 2 is a party response, not an independent regulator's adjudication of the disputes.

The proponent's assertions that there were no observed impacts outside the containment-cell boundary, that the wellfield is not hydraulically connected, that the alternative site is unsuitable, and that the project is protective of health/environment must each be tested against the underlying monitoring data, source reports, assumptions and regulatory records.

## 15. Conclusions limited to the reviewed record

1. The Province's 2022 assessment was a site-specific feasibility assessment of the Mount William parcel group identified by PIDs 00865469 and 00865485, supported by GHD and NSECC input. It was not a field-verified environmental site investigation.
2. Hive's 2023 report was a broader desktop screening and comparative siting exercise. It expressly conducted no field visits or studies and identified important limits in its private-land search.
3. The two reports use different scopes, parcel footprints and decision frames. Their conclusions cannot be compared without normalizing those differences.
4. The NPNS active Cell 3 finding is a desktop screening conclusion under Hive's selected criteria, not proof that the whole former mill site can accept Boat Harbour waste.
5. The groundwater relationship between the existing cell/Boat Harbour and PLFN's wellfield remains disputed. The original 2010 and 2016 reports and raw data are essential.
6. The Province's alternative-site cost and GHG arguments rely on estimates with explicit limitations. The GHG Annex 7 contains an apparent scenario-attribution and equivalency mismatch that must be resolved before its headline number is relied upon.
7. The 2019 land-use plan is evidence of planning work involving PLFN, but formal adoption by PLFN has not been established.
8. Nothing in this document determines Aboriginal title, the legality of consultation, the legal effect of any agreement, or the admissibility of evidence.

All conclusions above are limited to the records and text reviewed to date. No original PDF binary has yet been hashed in a controlled evidence vault and no independent second reviewer has signed this comparison.
